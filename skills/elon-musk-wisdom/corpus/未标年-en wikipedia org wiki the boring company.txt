**The Boring Company** (**TBC**) is an American infrastructure, [tunnel construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel_construction "Tunnel construction") service, and equipment company founded by [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk"). TBC was founded as a subsidiary of [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX") in 2017, and was [spun off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off "Corporate spin-off") as a separate corporation in 2018. TBC has completed multiple test tunnels and one tunneling project that is open to the public.

In 2018, TBC completed [one test tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boring_Test_Tunnel "Boring Test Tunnel") in [Los Angeles County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County,_California "Los Angeles County, California"). In 2021, TBC completed the [Las Vegas Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Convention_Center "Las Vegas Convention Center") (LVCC) [Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegas_Loop "Vegas Loop"), a three-station transportation system with 1.7 miles (2.7 km) of tunnels. As of April 2024, a segment to [Resorts World Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resorts_World_Las_Vegas "Resorts World Las Vegas") is also open, and tunnels to [Encore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encore_Las_Vegas "Encore Las Vegas") and [Westgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westgate_Las_Vegas "Westgate Las Vegas") resorts are being finalized. The system is planned to expand to a total of 68 miles (109 km) of tunnels. In October 2025, TBC began trialing [self-driving vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-driving_car "Self-driving car") with a safety driver on one section of the Las Vegas Loop.[\[4\]](#cite_note-4)

Many other TBC projects in cities across the United States were announced, but subsequently became inactive or were canceled.[\[5\]](#cite_note-wsj_Q115488224-5)[\[6\]](#cite_note-6)

Musk announced the idea of the Boring Company in December 2016,[\[7\]](#cite_note-Gajanan_2016_b795-7) and it was officially registered as "TBC – The Boring Company" on January 11, 2017.[\[8\]](#cite_note-8) Musk cited difficulty with Los Angeles traffic, and what he sees as limitations of its two-dimensional transportation network, as his early inspiration for the project.[\[9\]](#cite_note-Chafkin-9)[\[10\]](#cite_note-:10-10) The Boring Company was formed as a SpaceX [subsidiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiary "Subsidiary").[\[11\]](#cite_note-hawthore20180911-11) According to Musk, the company's goal is to enhance tunneling speed enough such that establishing a [tunnel network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel_network "Tunnel network") is financially feasible.[\[12\]](#cite_note-Thompson-12)[\[13\]](#cite_note-:1-13)

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Elon_Musk_Accelerates_the_Boring_%2845716125474%29.jpg/330px-Elon_Musk_Accelerates_the_Boring_%2845716125474%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Elon_Musk_Accelerates_the_Boring_\(45716125474\).jpg)

Elon Musk speaking at the inauguration of the test tunnel in [Hawthorne, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne,_California "Hawthorne, California"), December 2018

In early 2018, the Boring Company was [spun out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off "Corporate spin-off") from SpaceX and into a separate [corporate entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_entity "Corporate entity").[\[14\]](#cite_note-wsj20181217-14) Early employees came from a variety of backgrounds, including those from SpaceX. The company completed several tests in [Hawthorne, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne,_California "Hawthorne, California") and showcased a test tunnel on December 18, 2018.[\[15\]](#cite_note-15)

After raising US$113 million from Musk himself, supplemented by TBC merchandise sales during 2018,[\[16\]](#cite_note-bloomberg20190725-16) the Boring Company sold $120 million in stock to venture capital firms in July 2019.[\[17\]](#cite_note-17)

By November 2019, [Steve Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Davis_\(executive\) "Steve Davis (executive)") had become company president after leading efforts for Musk since 2016. Davis was one of the earliest hires at SpaceX (in 2003) and has master's degrees in particle physics and aerospace engineering, as well as degrees in finance and mechanical engineering.[\[18\]](#cite_note-bloomberg2019115-18)[\[19\]](#cite_note-19) In November 2020, TBC announced hiring for positions in Austin, Texas, and by December 2020 had leased two buildings in a 14-acre (5.7 ha) industrial complex northeast of Austin, approximately 16 miles (26 km) north of [Texas Gigafactory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giga_Texas "Giga Texas").[\[20\]](#cite_note-20)

On April 20, 2022, the company announced an additional $675 million Series C funding round, valuing the company at approximately $5.675 billion.[\[5\]](#cite_note-wsj_Q115488224-5) The round was led by Vy Capital and [Sequoia Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequoia_Capital "Sequoia Capital"),[\[5\]](#cite_note-wsj_Q115488224-5) with participation from [Valor Equity Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valor_Equity_Partners "Valor Equity Partners"), [Founders Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founders_Fund "Founders Fund"), [8VC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8VC "8VC"), [Craft Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_Ventures "Craft Ventures"), and [DFJ Growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFJ_Growth_Fund "DFJ Growth Fund"). In 2022, the company was cited by the [Texas Commission on Environmental Quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Commission_on_Environmental_Quality "Texas Commission on Environmental Quality") for five violations of Texas environmental regulations.[\[21\]](#cite_note-21)

Sometime before April 2023, the company moved their headquarters and engineering facilities to [Bastrop, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastrop,_Texas "Bastrop, Texas"),[\[1\]](#cite_note-nce20230403-1) approximately 25 miles (40 km) east of Texas Gigafactory.

Musk has previously addressed the question of whether the company would use its technology to build a [base on Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization_of_Mars "Colonization of Mars") to avoid [radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_ionizing_radiation_in_spaceflight "Effects of ionizing radiation in spaceflight").[\[22\]](#cite_note-22) While SpaceX President and COO Gwynne Shotwell stated that such an application could be a good idea, Musk also has suggested that the company's tunneling technology could potentially be used in the development of future settlements on Mars.[\[23\]](#cite_note-23)[\[24\]](#cite_note-24)

In 2018, the company began offering 20,000 "flamethrowers" for preordering.[\[25\]](#cite_note-Horowitz_2018_j526-25) The "flamethrower" was a [blow torch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blow_torch "Blow torch") shaped to look like a gun and is legal in all U.S. states except Maryland.[\[26\]](#cite_note-26) All 20,000 "flamethrowers" were sold in just a few days.[\[27\]](#cite_note-Gibbs_2018_c919-27) After customs officials said that they would not allow imports of any items called "flamethrowers", Musk announced that he would rename them to "Not-A-Flamethrower" since the devices were in fact akin to roofing torches. Musk announced separate sales of a fire extinguisher, which he described as "overpriced... but this one comes with a cool sticker".[\[28\]](#cite_note-28)

### Not-a-Boring Competition

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Not-a-Boring Competition")\]

In 2020, TBC released rules for a student tunnel-boring competition. The first competition was held in Las Vegas in September 2021.[\[29\]](#cite_note-intengineering20210923-29) Officially named the Not-a-Boring Competition, the challenge was to "quickly and accurately drill a tunnel that was 30 m-long (98 ft) and 30 cm-wide (0.98 ft)".[\[29\]](#cite_note-intengineering20210923-29)

Applications were received from 400 potential participants. A technical design review left 12 teams that were invited to Las Vegas to demonstrate their engineering solution in a September 2021 competition. The winning team was _TUM Boring_ from [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich") who managed to excavate a 22 m (72 ft) bore while meeting the requisite safety requirements. _TUM Boring_ used a conventional [pipe jacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_jacking "Pipe jacking") method to build the tunnel, but employed a novel revolving pipe storage design to minimize downtime between pipe segments.[\[29\]](#cite_note-intengineering20210923-29)

A second competition was held in April 2023. New contest criteria required a 30 m (98 ft)\-long 500 mm (20 in)\-diameter, this time with a turn radius. Five teams from four countries—the United States, Germany, United Kingdom, and Switzerland—made the finals and journeyed to Texas to compete. _TUM Boring_ again won with a design that reached a maximum velocity of 7 mm/s (0.28 in/s).[\[1\]](#cite_note-nce20230403-1) _Swissloop Tunneling_ finished second overall and won the innovation award.[\[30\]](#cite_note-Numbers_2023_p313-30)

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/The_new_TBM_at_The_Boring_Company_%2848108809063%29.jpg/330px-The_new_TBM_at_The_Boring_Company_%2848108809063%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_new_TBM_at_The_Boring_Company_\(48108809063\).jpg)

The boring machine in June 2019

TBC's first boring machine was Godot, a conventional [tunnel boring machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel_boring_machine "Tunnel boring machine") (TBM) made by [Lovat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caterpillar_Inc. "Caterpillar Inc.").[\[31\]](#cite_note-:0-31)[\[32\]](#cite_note-Loveday_2017_e553-32)

Godot was followed by Prufrock.[\[33\]](#cite_note-wired2018-33) It introduced the principle of onsite upgrades.[\[34\]](#cite_note-auto3-34)[\[35\]](#cite_note-35)

Prufrock medium bore 1 (MB1) introduced [porpoising](#Porpoising), [continuous mining](#Continuous_mining), rapid iteration, and remote operation (termed "zero people in tunnel" (ZPIT)). It was the first TBC machine to tunnel through rock (limestone). Its [cutter head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel_boring_machine#Types "Tunnel boring machine") featured 15.5-inch disc cutters.[\[36\]](#cite_note-:8-36)[\[37\]](#cite_note-:3-37)

The company said in 2022 that the goal of MB2 was to dig up to a mile per week, which is six times faster than Godot.[\[36\]](#cite_note-:8-36)[\[34\]](#cite_note-auto3-34)[\[38\]](#cite_note-38) Compared to MB1, it offered 15% higher power and 1.5-inch larger disc cutters (which were 17-inch).[\[37\]](#cite_note-:3-37)

Prufrock-MB2 arrived in Nashville, Tennessee, in April 2026 and completed testing in June 2026.[\[39\]](#cite_note-39)

The company said in 2022 that the goal of MB3 was to dig up to seven miles per day.[\[34\]](#cite_note-auto3-34) As of June 2026, MB3 is being built and specifications are not available.[\[37\]](#cite_note-:3-37)

Liner Truck 3 was introduced in 2026 and transports liner segments from the surface to the TBM. It is remotely piloted and has a low profile to allow it to drive under the conveyor belt. [\[40\]](#cite_note-40) It has a range of 28 miles and can carry 22 tons of concrete liner segments at once.[\[41\]](#cite_note-41)

The goal is to replace tunnel entry and exit excavations by having the TBM "porpoise" in and out of the ground. The TBM is trucked in and placed at an angle to the ground. (Prufrock 2 and 3 required an earthen ramp to set it at the correct angle before beginning to tunnel). It then bores into the ground and changes angles as it continues boring, eventually returning to the surface and being loaded onto the truck.[\[42\]](#cite_note-:11-42)

In conventional systems, one large excavation is made at the tunnel entrance to allow the TBM to be lowered to the tunnel depth and assembled. A similar excavation is made at the tunnel exit to allow the TBM to be disassembled and lifted out.[\[42\]](#cite_note-:11-42)

TBC lines its tunnels with hexagonal concrete segments. It installs the segments via an electric, autonomous, process.[\[43\]](#cite_note-43) Segments arrive in the tunnel via an electric wheeled liner truck powered by motors and batteries from [Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla,_Inc. "Tesla, Inc.").[\[44\]](#cite_note-r399-44)

### Continuous tunneling

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Continuous tunneling")\]

TBC is working to install ring liners without stopping tunneling. Conventional systems stop every five feet or so to install another segment of the tunnel lining, and to extend the rail line. The goal is to increase tunneling time per day from 11 hours to 24 hours.[\[45\]](#cite_note-:14-45)

An electrically-powered conveyor belt (Vertical Cassette, or VC) is used to transport excavated material ("muck") out of the tunnel. The conveyor belt is positioned at the top of the tunnel and is attached to the concrete lining segments. [\[46\]](#cite_note-46)

VC7 can extract 990 tons/hour at 50% capacity, supporting a 4 mile/week tunneling speed. The belt travels 660 ft/minute. VC7 can handle curves with radius as small as 500 ft. Individual units can be chained together every 1.5 miles to support long tunnels. VCs co-operate in a tunnel with the liner truck.[\[47\]](#cite_note-47)

### Hawthorne test tunnel

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Hawthorne test tunnel")\]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Reinventing_Boring_%2844544295190%29.jpg/330px-Reinventing_Boring_%2844544295190%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Reinventing_Boring_\(44544295190\).jpg)

Tunnel built in Hawthorne, December 2018

The [Hawthorne test tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_test_tunnel "Hawthorne test tunnel"), or Boring test tunnel, is a 1.14 miles (1.83 km) tunnel in [Hawthorne, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne,_California "Hawthorne, California"), intended for testing “tunneling and public transportation systems”,[\[48\]](#cite_note-48) constructed between 2016 and 2018.[\[49\]](#cite_note-Hawthorne_test_tunnel_artsy-2022-12-0822-49)[\[50\]](#cite_note-Hawthorne_test_tunnel_mcfarland-2025-12-192-50) It was accompanied by a mile-long aboveground tube on nearby Jack Northrop Ave designed for testing hyperloop and loop-based transportation, which was used for student competitions in 2018 and 2019 before being dismantled in 2022.[\[51\]](#cite_note-51)[\[52\]](#cite_note-52)

The [Vegas Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegas_Loop "Vegas Loop"), originally known as the Las Vegas Convention Center Loop (LVCC Loop), is a car tunnel system that serves the [Las Vegas Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Convention_Center "Las Vegas Convention Center") and area hotels. Operating since 2021, the system uses [Tesla Model Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_Y "Tesla Model Y") vehicles to shuttle passengers among nine stations. The Boring Company began construction in November 2019[\[53\]](#cite_note-Vegas_Loop_NBC-53) and has continued intermittent tunnel drilling for planned stations.[\[54\]](#cite_note-Vegas_Loop_8news-54) When completed, the company projects that the Loop will include 68 miles of tunnels and 123 stations.[\[55\]](#cite_note-55)

The [Dubai Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Loop "Dubai Loop") is a car tunnel system project that aims to serve [Downtown Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Dubai "Downtown Dubai"). It was first announced in February 2025.[\[56\]](#cite_note-56) Construction started in February 2026.[\[57\]](#cite_note-57) It is built by The Boring Company. The system will use [Tesla Model Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_Y "Tesla Model Y") vehicles to shuttle passengers.

The first phase of the project will cover 6.4 kilometres (4.0 mi), comprising four stations ([Dubai International Financial Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_International_Financial_Centre "Dubai International Financial Centre"), ICD Brookfield Place, Zaabeel Dubai Mall Parking, and [Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burj_Khalifa/Dubai_Mall_\(Dubai_Metro\) "Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall (Dubai Metro)")), transporting 13,000 passengers per day and shortening the travel time between DIFC and [Dubai Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Mall "Dubai Mall") from 20 to 3 minutes.[\[58\]](#cite_note-58)[\[59\]](#cite_note-Dubai_Loop_:0-59) It is expected to be launched by 2027.[\[59\]](#cite_note-Dubai_Loop_:0-59) The second phase will feature 19 stations across 22.2 kilometres (13.8 mi), connecting [Dubai World Trade Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_World_Trade_Centre "Dubai World Trade Centre") to [Business Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Bay "Business Bay"). It is planned to be built within three years and handle 30,000 passengers per day.[\[59\]](#cite_note-Dubai_Loop_:0-59)

In 2025, the [Shingle Creek Transit and Utility Community Development District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shingle_Creek_Transit_and_Utility_Community_Development_District "Shingle Creek Transit and Utility Community Development District") began exploring proposals for a transportation system linking the [Epic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Universe "Epic Universe") theme park with other [Universal Orlando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Orlando "Universal Orlando") properties along [International Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Drive "International Drive") and near [Interstate 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_4 "Interstate 4").[\[63\]](#cite_note-63) In February 2026, the district voted to begin contract negotiations with The Boring Company regarding a proposed underground transit system modeled after the Vegas Loop.[\[64\]](#cite_note-64)[\[65\]](#cite_note-65)[\[66\]](#cite_note-66)

## Inactive and cancelled projects

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Inactive and cancelled projects")\]

#### Washington, DC to Baltimore, Maryland

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Washington, DC to Baltimore, Maryland")\]

In 2017, Musk announced plans to build a Hyperloop connecting Washington, DC to Baltimore.[\[67\]](#cite_note-67) This was supplanted in 2018 by a proposal to build a route following the [Baltimore–Washington Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore–Washington_Parkway "Baltimore–Washington Parkway").[\[68\]](#cite_note-68) The Maryland Transportation Authority officially approved the project.[\[69\]](#cite_note-69) In 2019, a draft [Environmental Assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Assessment "Environmental Assessment") for the project was completed.[\[70\]](#cite_note-Snyder_2019_h030-70) As of 2021, the project was no longer listed on the company website, the federal government had received no indication the company wanted to move forward, TBC dropped a Maryland lobbyist it had hired for the project, and the company declined to comment on the project.[\[71\]](#cite_note-:2-71)

In 2018, the company won a bid to build a high-speed link from [downtown Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Chicago "Downtown Chicago") to [O'Hare Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Hare_International_Airport "O'Hare International Airport"), to be known as the [Chicago Express Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Express_Loop "Chicago Express Loop").[\[72\]](#cite_note-suntimes20180320-72)[\[73\]](#cite_note-73)[\[74\]](#cite_note-74) As of 2021, the plan had been dropped.[\[75\]](#cite_note-nbc20210608-75)

#### Los Angeles, California

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Los Angeles, California")\]

In 2018, TBC proposed to develop a 2.7-mile-long (4.3 km) test tunnel on a north–south alignment parallel to [Interstate 405](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_405_\(California\) "Interstate 405 (California)") and adjacent to Sepulveda Boulevard.[\[76\]](#cite_note-musk20180517-76): 25:50  Public opposition and [lawsuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawsuit "Lawsuit") led the company to abandon the idea.[\[77\]](#cite_note-tc20181128-77)[\[78\]](#cite_note-78) Also in 2018, the company proposed to build a 3.6-mile (5.8 km) tunnel called the "Dugout Loop" from [Vermont Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermont_Avenue "Vermont Avenue") to [Dodger Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodger_Stadium "Dodger Stadium"). As of June 2021, the project had been removed from TBC's website.[\[75\]](#cite_note-nbc20210608-75)

#### San Jose, California

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: San Jose, California")\]

In 2019, a link between [San Jose International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_International_Airport "San Jose International Airport") and [Diridon station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Diridon_station "San Jose Diridon station"), was discussed as an alternative to an $800 million traditional rail link.[\[79\]](#cite_note-79) Plans were later dropped.[\[80\]](#cite_note-80)

#### San Bernardino County, California

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: San Bernardino County, California")\]

In February 2021, the [San Bernardino County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_County,_California "San Bernardino County, California") Transportation Authority (SBCTA) in California approved beginning contract negotiations with TBC to build a nearly 4-mile (6.4 km) tunnel connecting the [Ontario airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_International_Airport "Ontario International Airport") with the [Rancho Cucamonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Cucamonga,_California "Rancho Cucamonga, California") [Metrolink/Future Brightline West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\) "Metrolink (California)") train station.[\[81\]](#cite_note-81) However, TBC did not submit a proposal after a third party was involved to study the project impacts.[\[82\]](#cite_note-82) As of 2022, the SBCTA has plans to build the tunnel system using "another company more familiar with the state's bureaucracy to do the [Environmental Impact Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Impact_Report "Environmental Impact Report")".[\[83\]](#cite_note-83)

#### Fort Lauderdale, Florida

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Fort Lauderdale, Florida")\]

In July 2021, Fort Lauderdale, Florida, accepted a proposal from the Boring Company for a tunnel between downtown and the beach, to be dubbed the "[Las Olas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Olas_Boulevard "Las Olas Boulevard") Loop".[\[84\]](#cite_note-:4-84) In August 2021, the city was beginning final negotiations with TBC,[\[85\]](#cite_note-:5-85) and Mayor [Dean Trantalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Trantalis "Dean Trantalis") estimated the total cost of the 5-mile (8.0 km) round-trip tunnel would be between $90 and $100 million, including stations.[\[86\]](#cite_note-86) As of December 2022, the city suspended efforts to continue the project.[\[87\]](#cite_note-:12-87)

[Bexar County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bexar_County "Bexar County") selected The Boring Company to build a transit link between downtown [San Antonio, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio,_Texas "San Antonio, Texas") and [San Antonio International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_International_Airport "San Antonio International Airport") in March 2022, but was then "ghosted" by the company.[\[88\]](#cite_note-88)

In February 2021, [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami "Miami") mayor [Francis Suarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Suarez "Francis Suarez") revealed that Musk had proposed to dig a two-mile tunnel under the [Miami River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_River_\(Florida\) "Miami River (Florida)") for $30 million, within a six-month timescale, compared with $1 billion over four years estimated by the local transit authority. Much of the savings would be achieved by simplifying ventilation systems and allowing only electric vehicles.[\[89\]](#cite_note-89) As of November 2023, the city is waiting for the Miami Dade Transportation Planning Organization to complete an analysis of the project.[\[87\]](#cite_note-:12-87)

#### Cameron County, Texas

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Boring_Company&action=edit&section=33 "Edit section: Cameron County, Texas")\]

In August 2021, a preliminary concept discussion was held with officials of [Cameron County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_County,_Texas "Cameron County, Texas") on the potential construction of a tunnel from [South Padre Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Padre_Island "South Padre Island") to [Boca Chica Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Chica_Beach "Boca Chica Beach") in [South Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Texas "South Texas"). If built, the tunnel would be required to pass beneath the [Brownsville Ship Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsville_Ship_Channel "Brownsville Ship Channel").[\[90\]](#cite_note-rgvnes20210822-90) It would allow [SpaceX's facilities at Starbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starbase "SpaceX Starbase") to remain accessible if [Highway 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_4 "Texas State Highway 4"), its sole access road, was closed.[\[91\]](#cite_note-Port_Isabel-South_Padre_Press_2021_m048-91)

In January 2019, Musk responded to a tweet by an Australian member of parliament regarding a tunnel through the [Blue Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Mountains_\(New_South_Wales\) "Blue Mountains (New South Wales)") to the west of Sydney, suggesting costs of $750 million for a 31-mile (50 km) tunnel, plus $50 million per station.[\[92\]](#cite_note-92) New South Wales' transportation department found that based on the cost of the Hawthorne tunnel, the estimated cost would be closer to $6 billion.[\[93\]](#cite_note-93)[\[94\]](#cite_note-94)

Civil engineering experts and tunneling industry veterans questioned whether TBC could render tunnels faster and cheaper than competitors. _Tunnelling Journal_ dismissed the company as a "vanity project".[\[95\]](#cite_note-NBC_News_2021_b862-95) The low capacity of TBC tunnels make them inefficient compared to existing public transit, with only a fraction of the capacity of a conventional rapid-transit subway.[\[96\]](#cite_note-96)[\[97\]](#cite_note-97)[\[98\]](#cite_note-:6-98)[\[99\]](#cite_note-:7-99)

Musk's planned tunnels were criticized for lacking such safety features as emergency exit corridors, ventilation systems, or fire suppression. In addition, the single lane tunnels left it impossible for vehicles to pass one another in the event of collision, mechanical failure, or other traffic obstruction, and instead would shut down the entire tunnel section.[\[100\]](#cite_note-100)[\[101\]](#cite_note-101)

James Moore, director of transportation engineering at the University of Southern California, said that "there are cheaper ways to provide better transportation for large numbers of people", such as managing traffic with tolls.[\[99\]](#cite_note-:7-99) Public transit consultant [Jarrett Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarrett_Walker "Jarrett Walker") called TBC "wildly hyped", and criticized how the company "dazzled city governments and investors with visions of an efficient subway where you never have to get out of your car, \[but turned\] out to be a paved road tunnel".[\[98\]](#cite_note-:6-98)[\[102\]](#cite_note-Tangermann_2023_j035-102)
