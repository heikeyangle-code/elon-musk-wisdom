**OpenAI** is an American [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") (AI) [public benefit corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_benefit_corporation "Public benefit corporation") (PBC) headquartered in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), California. It develops [proprietary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_software "Proprietary software") [generative AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_AI "Generative AI") models, particularly its [generative pre-trained transformer (GPT) series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Products_and_applications_of_OpenAI#Text_generation "Products and applications of OpenAI") of [large language models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model"). Its release of [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT") in November 2022 has been credited with catalyzing the [AI boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_boom "AI boom"); as of September 2026, ChatGPT is the [fifth-most-visited website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-visited_websites "List of most-visited websites") globally.[\[9\]](#cite_note-10)[\[10\]](#cite_note-11) OpenAI also releases the [GPT Image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT_Image "GPT Image") models and [Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_Codex_\(AI_agent\) "OpenAI Codex (AI agent)"), an [AI coding agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-assisted_software_development "AI-assisted software development"). In March 2026, OpenAI closed a funding round with a [post-money valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-money_valuation "Post-money valuation") of US$852 billion,[\[11\]](#cite_note-:3-12) making it one of the most valuable AI [pure play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_play "Pure play") companies in the world, rivalled only by [Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic "Anthropic").[\[12\]](#cite_note-13)

OpenAI was founded in 2015 as a [nonprofit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonprofit_organization "Nonprofit organization"), with [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") and [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman "Sam Altman") as co-chairs; Musk resigned in 2018. A for-profit subsidiary was created in 2019, and a 2025 restructuring converted the subsidiary into OpenAI Group PBC, that is 26% owned by the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation. [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") has invested over $13 billion into OpenAI.[\[13\]](#cite_note-Reuters_2025-14) OpenAI partners with the US government via the [Stargate Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_LLC "Stargate LLC") infrastructure venture, and with the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense"), [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory "Los Alamos National Laboratory"), and [arms company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_industry "Arms industry") [Anduril Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anduril_Industries "Anduril Industries"). Former OpenAI personnel have founded competing AI companies including Anthropic, [Safe Superintelligence Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_Superintelligence_Inc. "Safe Superintelligence Inc."), [SpaceXAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceXAI "SpaceXAI"), and [Thinking Machines Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking_Machines_Lab "Thinking Machines Lab").[\[14\]](#cite_note-:4-15)

OpenAI has faced multiple lawsuits for alleged [deaths linked to its chatbots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaths_linked_to_chatbots "Deaths linked to chatbots") and [copyright infringement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_infringement "Copyright infringement") against authors and media companies. In November 2023, OpenAI's board [removed Sam Altman as CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Removal_of_Sam_Altman_from_OpenAI "Removal of Sam Altman from OpenAI"), citing a lack of confidence, but reinstated him five days later after a board reconstruction. In 2024, roughly half of OpenAI's [AI safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_safety "AI safety") researchers left the company, often citing the company's deprioritization of safety.[\[15\]](#cite_note-16)[\[16\]](#cite_note-17) Between May and July 2026, OpenAI [agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_agent "AI agent") gained [unintended internet access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_capability_control#Escaping_containment "AI capability control") and conducted autonomous hacks, including [against Hugging Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI–HuggingFace_incident "OpenAI–HuggingFace incident") and [against Australia's Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_OpenAI_infiltration_of_Medicare "2026 OpenAI infiltration of Medicare"). In September, OpenAI published a claimed proof regarding [Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier–Stokes_existence_and_smoothness "Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness"), a [Millennium Prize Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Prize_Problems "Millennium Prize Problems") in mathematics, causing a [controversy over priority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier–Stokes_priority_controversy "Navier–Stokes priority controversy").

## Founding

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Pioneer_Building%2C_San_Francisco_%282019%29_-1.jpg/250px-Pioneer_Building%2C_San_Francisco_%282019%29_-1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pioneer_Building,_San_Francisco_\(2019\)_-1.jpg)

Former headquarters at the [Pioneer Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Building_\(San_Francisco\) "Pioneer Building (San Francisco)") in San Francisco

In December 2015, OpenAI was founded as the [nonprofit organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonprofit_organization "Nonprofit organization") OpenAI, Inc.,[\[17\]](#cite_note-Ostrovsky-2025-18) by [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk"), [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman "Sam Altman"), [Ilya Sutskever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Sutskever "Ilya Sutskever"), [Greg Brockman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Brockman "Greg Brockman"), [Trevor Blackwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Blackwell "Trevor Blackwell"), Vicki Cheung, [Andrej Karpathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrej_Karpathy "Andrej Karpathy"), [Durk Kingma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durk_Kingma "Durk Kingma"), [John Schulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Schulman "John Schulman"), Pamela Vagata, and [Wojciech Zaremba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Zaremba "Wojciech Zaremba"), with Musk and Altman as the co-chairs.[\[18\]](#cite_note-wired_inside-19)[\[19\]](#cite_note-20) A total of $1 billion in capital was pledged by Musk, Altman, Greg Brockman, [Reid Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reid_Hoffman "Reid Hoffman"), [Jessica Livingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Livingston "Jessica Livingston"), [Peter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thiel "Peter Thiel"), [Amazon Web Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services "Amazon Web Services") (AWS), and [Infosys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys "Infosys").[\[20\]](#cite_note-21)[\[21\]](#cite_note-22) However, the actual capital collected significantly lagged pledges. According to tax filings, only $133 million had been received by 2021.[\[22\]](#cite_note-23)

Musk stated in 2015 that he was partly motivated by concerns about [AI safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_safety "AI safety") and [existential risk from artificial general intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_risk_from_artificial_general_intelligence "Existential risk from artificial general intelligence").[\[23\]](#cite_note-csmonitor-24)[\[18\]](#cite_note-wired_inside-19) OpenAI stated that "it's hard to fathom how much human-level AI could benefit society", and that it is equally difficult to comprehend "how much it could damage society if built or used incorrectly".[\[24\]](#cite_note-bbc-giants-25) The startup also wrote that AI "should be an extension of individual human wills and, in the spirit of liberty, as broadly and evenly distributed as possible",[\[24\]](#cite_note-bbc-giants-25) and that "because of AI's surprising history, it's hard to predict when human-level AI might come within reach. When it does, it'll be important to have a leading research institution which can prioritize a good outcome for all over its own self-interest."[\[25\]](#cite_note-26) Altman expected a decades-long project that eventually surpasses human intelligence.[\[26\]](#cite_note-wired_far_more-27)

In its founding charter, OpenAI defined its mission as ensuring that [artificial general intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence "Artificial general intelligence") (AGI) "benefits all of humanity",[\[27\]](#cite_note-Booth-2025-28) and stated an intention to collaborate openly with other institutions by making certain patents and research publicly available, but later restricted access to its most capable models, citing competitive and safety concerns.[\[28\]](#cite_note-29)[\[24\]](#cite_note-bbc-giants-25)[\[18\]](#cite_note-wired_inside-19) OpenAI was initially run from Brockman's living room.[\[29\]](#cite_note-30) In September 2016, it moved to the [Pioneer Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Building_\(San_Francisco\) "Pioneer Building (San Francisco)") in the [Mission District, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_District,_San_Francisco "Mission District, San Francisco").[\[30\]](#cite_note-31)[\[31\]](#cite_note-technologyreview-32)[\[32\]](#cite_note-33)

Brockman met with [Yoshua Bengio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshua_Bengio "Yoshua Bengio"), one of the "founding fathers" of [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning "Deep learning"), and drew up a list of great AI researchers.[\[18\]](#cite_note-wired_inside-19) Brockman was able to hire nine of them as the first employees in December 2015.[\[18\]](#cite_note-wired_inside-19) OpenAI did not pay AI researchers salaries comparable to those of [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") or [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google").[\[18\]](#cite_note-wired_inside-19) It also did not pay stock options which AI researchers typically get. Nevertheless, OpenAI spent $7 million on its first 52 employees in 2016.[\[33\]](#cite_note-salaryTimes-34) OpenAI's potential and mission drew these researchers to the firm; a Google employee said he was willing to leave Google for OpenAI "partly because of the very strong group of people and, to a very large extent, because of its mission."[\[18\]](#cite_note-wired_inside-19) OpenAI co-founder [Wojciech Zaremba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Zaremba "Wojciech Zaremba") stated that he turned down "borderline crazy" offers of two to three times his market value to join OpenAI instead.[\[18\]](#cite_note-wired_inside-19)

In April 2016, OpenAI released a public beta of "OpenAI Gym", its platform for [reinforcement learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement_learning "Reinforcement learning") research.[\[34\]](#cite_note-Dave_Gershgorn-2016-35) [Nvidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia "Nvidia") gifted its first [DGX-1 supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX "Nvidia DGX") to OpenAI in August 2016 to help it train larger and more complex AI models with the capability of reducing processing time from six days to two hours.[\[35\]](#cite_note-36)[\[36\]](#cite_note-37) In December 2016, OpenAI released "Universe", a software platform for measuring and training an AI's general intelligence across the world's supply of games, websites, and other applications.[\[37\]](#cite_note-38)[\[38\]](#cite_note-39)[\[39\]](#cite_note-40)[\[40\]](#cite_note-41)

## Corporate structure

### Transition from nonprofit

#### Creation of for-profit subsidiaries

In 2019, OpenAI transitioned from nonprofit to "capped" for-profit, with the profit being capped at 100 times any investment.[\[41\]](#cite_note-42) OpenAI formed three [subsidiaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiaries "Subsidiaries"): the [holding company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_company "Holding company") OpenAI LP, the holding company OpenAI GP LLC, and the capped for-profit company OpenAI Global, LLC. The holding company OpenAI LP was owned by the nonprofit OpenAI, Inc., OpenAI employees, and investors, whereas OpenAI Global, LLC, was owned by OpenAI LP and Microsoft, the latter of which had a minority non-voting stake. The nonprofit OpenAI, Inc., controlled OpenAI GP LLC, which in turn controlled OpenAI LP and OpenAI Global, LLC.[\[42\]](#cite_note-Fan-2025-43): 1050–1051 

OpenAI stated that the capped-profit model would allow it to legally attract investment from venture funds and compensate employees with [equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_\(finance\) "Equity (finance)") as competing AI companies did.[\[43\]](#cite_note-wired_investors-44)[\[44\]](#cite_note-bloomberg_arm-45) Microsoft subsequently invested $1 billion in OpenAI LP, and OpenAI's services were migrated to [Microsoft Azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Azure "Microsoft Azure").[\[45\]](#cite_note-Foley-2019-46) Since then, OpenAI systems have run on an Azure-based [supercomputing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputing "Supercomputing") platform.[\[46\]](#cite_note-Foley_2020-47)[\[47\]](#cite_note-Engadget_2020-48)

The 2019 restructuring made the nonprofit OpenAI, Inc., the only [controlling shareholder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlling_interest "Controlling interest") of OpenAI LP, which retained a formal [fiduciary responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiduciary_duty "Fiduciary duty") to OpenAI, Inc.'s nonprofit charter. A majority of OpenAI, Inc.'s board was barred from having financial stakes in OpenAI LP.[\[43\]](#cite_note-wired_investors-44) In addition, minority members with a stake in OpenAI LP were barred from certain votes due to conflict of interest.[\[44\]](#cite_note-bloomberg_arm-45) Some researchers have argued that OpenAI's switch to for-profit status was inconsistent with OpenAI's claims to be "democratizing" AI.[\[48\]](#cite_note-49)

#### Conversion to public benefit corporation

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/OpenAI_corporate_structure_revised.svg/250px-OpenAI_corporate_structure_revised.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:OpenAI_corporate_structure_revised.svg)

OpenAI's corporate structure, as of May 2026

In December 2024, OpenAI proposed a restructuring plan to convert the capped-profit into a Delaware-based [public benefit corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_benefit_corporation "Public benefit corporation") (PBC), remove its profit cap, and release it from the control of the nonprofit.[\[42\]](#cite_note-Fan-2025-43): 1053  The nonprofit would sell its control and other assets, getting equity in return, and would use it to fund and pursue separate charitable projects, including in science and education. OpenAI's leadership described the change as necessary to secure additional investments, and stated that the nonprofit's founding mission to ensure AGI "benefits all of humanity" would be better fulfilled.[\[27\]](#cite_note-Booth-2025-28)

The plan has been criticized by former employees. A legal letter named "Not For Private Gain" asked the [attorneys general of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_California "Attorney General of California") and [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Delaware "Attorney General of Delaware") to intervene, stating that the restructuring is illegal and would remove governance safeguards from the nonprofit and the attorneys general.[\[49\]](#cite_note-50)[\[50\]](#cite_note-51) The letter argues that OpenAI's original complex structure was deliberately designed to remain accountable to its mission, without the conflicting pressure of maximizing profits. It contends that the nonprofit is best positioned to advance its mission of ensuring AGI benefits all of humanity by continuing to control OpenAI Global, LLC, whatever the amount of equity that it could get in exchange.[\[51\]](#cite_note-Piper-2025-52) PBCs can choose how they balance their mission with profit-making. Controlling shareholders have a large influence on how closely a PBC sticks to its mission.[\[52\]](#cite_note-Reuters-2025-53)[\[51\]](#cite_note-Piper-2025-52) OpenAI subpoenaed six nonprofit organizations that opposed its for-profit transition, which the organizations described as an attempt to silence them.[\[53\]](#cite_note-Perlo-2025-54)[\[54\]](#cite_note-Shugerman-2025-55)

On October 28, 2025, OpenAI announced that it had adopted the new PBC corporate structure after receiving approval from the attorneys general of California and Delaware.[\[7\]](#cite_note-nyt-restructure-8) Under the new structure, OpenAI's for-profit branch became a PBC known as OpenAI Group PBC, while the nonprofit was renamed to the OpenAI Foundation.[\[7\]](#cite_note-nyt-restructure-8)[\[55\]](#cite_note-reuters-restructure-56) The OpenAI Foundation holds a 26% stake in the PBC, while Microsoft holds a 27% stake and the remaining 47% is owned by employees and other investors.[\[7\]](#cite_note-nyt-restructure-8)[\[56\]](#cite_note-cnbc-restructure-57)

All members of the OpenAI Group PBC board of directors are to be appointed by the OpenAI Foundation, which can remove them at any time.[\[7\]](#cite_note-nyt-restructure-8) As of April 2026, all but one of the board members of the OpenAI Foundation were also board members of OpenAI Group PBC.[\[57\]](#cite_note-Farrow-2026-58) In April 2026, co-founder Musk revised [his lawsuit against OpenAI and Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musk_v._Altman "Musk v. Altman") to seek the reversal of OpenAI's corporate restructuring.[\[58\]](#cite_note-Belanger-2026-59) Musk lost the lawsuit in May 2026, with the jury concluding that he waited beyond the three-year [statute of limitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_limitations "Statute of limitations") to sue OpenAI.[\[59\]](#cite_note-60)

Altman stated that an [initial public offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") (IPO) was the most likely path forward.[\[55\]](#cite_note-reuters-restructure-56) On June 8, 2026, OpenAI confirmed it filed for an IPO with the US [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Commission "Securities and Exchange Commission") (SEC),[\[60\]](#cite_note-61) but stated "it could be a while because there are things we want to do that are likely easier as a private company."[\[61\]](#cite_note-62) Altman confirmed in September that the IPO was delayed until 2027.[\[62\]](#cite_note-63)

### Partnership with Microsoft

Following its original $1 billion investment in 2019 which began an [AI boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_boom "AI boom"),[\[63\]](#cite_note-64)[\[64\]](#cite_note-65) Microsoft invested another $2 billion through 2022.[\[65\]](#cite_note-66) On January 23, 2023, Microsoft announced an additional $10 billion investment in OpenAI over multiple years, of which a significant portion would be used to purchase compute on [Microsoft Azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Azure "Microsoft Azure").[\[66\]](#cite_note-67)[\[67\]](#cite_note-68) These investments were made at a $29 billion valuation, double OpenAI's 2021 valuation.[\[68\]](#cite_note-69) Later that year, Microsoft began using OpenAI models for its [Copilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Copilot "Microsoft Copilot") chatbot, integrated Copilot into [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows "Microsoft Windows"), and released mobile Copilot apps.[\[69\]](#cite_note-unify-70)[\[70\]](#cite_note-androidlaunch-71)[\[71\]](#cite_note-ioslaunch-72)

Following OpenAI's 2025 restructuring, Microsoft owned a 27% stake in the for-profit OpenAI Group PBC, valued at $135 billion.[\[56\]](#cite_note-cnbc-restructure-57) In a deal announced the same day, OpenAI agreed to purchase $250 billion of Azure services, with Microsoft ceding their [right of first refusal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_first_refusal "Right of first refusal") over OpenAI's future cloud computing purchases.[\[56\]](#cite_note-cnbc-restructure-57)[\[72\]](#cite_note-verge-restructure-73) As part of the deal, OpenAI will continue to share 20% of its revenue with Microsoft until it achieves AGI, which must now be verified by an independent panel of experts.[\[55\]](#cite_note-reuters-restructure-56)[\[72\]](#cite_note-verge-restructure-73) The deal also loosened restrictions on both companies working with third parties, allowing Microsoft to pursue AGI independently and allowing OpenAI to develop products with other companies.[\[56\]](#cite_note-cnbc-restructure-57)[\[7\]](#cite_note-nyt-restructure-8)[\[72\]](#cite_note-verge-restructure-73)

In April 2026, the two firms announced a new agreement which removed many of the more restrictive aspects of their partnership. No longer the exclusive cloud provider, Microsoft then had the first right to provide cloud services, but OpenAI could purchase other services in the event that Microsoft did not provide sufficient capacity. OpenAI will no longer receive a portion of Microsoft's revenue from using OpenAI's technology. The clause, which created uncertainty for Microsoft in the event OpenAI determined that OpenAI had created AGI, was dropped.[\[73\]](#cite_note-74)

### Finances

In 2017, OpenAI spent $7.9 million, a quarter of its functional expenses, on cloud computing.[\[74\]](#cite_note-75) In comparison, [Google DeepMind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeepMind "DeepMind")'s total expenses in 2017 were $442 million. In the summer of 2018, training OpenAI's _Dota 2_ bots required renting 128,000 [CPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_processing_unit "Central processing unit") and 256 [GPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphics_processing_unit "Graphics processing unit") from Google for multiple weeks.[\[43\]](#cite_note-wired_investors-44)

In October 2024, OpenAI completed a $6.6 billion capital raise with a $157 billion valuation including investments from Microsoft, [Nvidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia "Nvidia"), and [SoftBank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoftBank_Group "SoftBank Group").[\[75\]](#cite_note-76)

In February 2026, OpenAI raised $110 billion at a $730 billion valuation, led by [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_\(company\) "Amazon (company)") ($50 billion), SoftBank ($30 billion), and Nvidia ($30 billion), surpassing the prior round as the largest private technology fundraise in history.[\[88\]](#cite_note-openai-110b-2026-89) The round was extended to $120 billion in March 2026.[\[89\]](#cite_note-openai-120b-2026-90) In April 2026, the company announced that it closed a funding round of $122 billion in committed capital at a post-money valuation of $852 billion.[\[11\]](#cite_note-:3-12)

## Business model

OpenAI uses a tiered revenue model that combines free consumer access, paid subscription services, enterprise licensing, and [application programming interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_programming_interface "Application programming interface") (API) usage-based pricing.[\[90\]](#cite_note-Friar2026-91) The basic functionality is provided at no cost, while advanced capabilities are offered through paid plans such as ChatGPT Plus and enterprise solutions. OpenAI's subscription and enterprise offerings are structured around scalable [compute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compute_\(machine_learning\) "Compute (machine learning)") usage and API integration into third-party platforms.[\[90\]](#cite_note-Friar2026-91) In 2025, the company reported significant revenue growth associated with expanded computing infrastructure and enterprise adoption.[\[91\]](#cite_note-ReutersRevenue2026-92)

The company's [cash burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_rate "Burn rate") remains high because of the intensive computational costs required to train and operate large language models. It projects an $8 billion operating loss in 2025.[\[92\]](#cite_note-93)[\[93\]](#cite_note-94) OpenAI reports revised long-term spending projections totaling approximately $115 billion through 2029,[\[94\]](#cite_note-yahoo-95) with annual expenditures projected to escalate significantly, reaching $17 billion in 2026, $35 billion in 2027, and $45 billion in 2028.[\[95\]](#cite_note-ground_news-96)[\[96\]](#cite_note-97) These expenditures are primarily allocated toward expanding compute infrastructure, developing proprietary AI chips, constructing data centers, and funding intensive model training programs, with more than half of the spending through the end of the decade expected to support research-intensive compute for model training and development.[\[97\]](#cite_note-98)

The company's financial strategy prioritizes market expansion and technological advancement over near-term profitability, with OpenAI's 2025 forecast predicting cash-flow-positive operations by 2029 and projecting revenue of approximately $200 billion by 2030.[\[95\]](#cite_note-ground_news-96)

In October 2025, OpenAI completed an employee share sale of up to $10 billion to existing investors which valued the company at $500 billion. The deal valued OpenAI as the most valuable privately owned company in the world—surpassing [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX") as the world's most valuable private company.[\[98\]](#cite_note-99)[\[99\]](#cite_note-100)

In April 2026, reports indicated that OpenAI projected approximately $2.5 billion in advertising revenue for the year, with estimates rising to $100 billion annually by 2030, highlighting advertising as a key component of its future business strategy.[\[100\]](#cite_note-101)

In June 2026, it was announced that OpenAI and the White House have been in ongoing talks for about a year to allow a possible government stake in the company.[\[101\]](#cite_note-102)

In August 2026, OpenAI said its advertising business had reached an annualized revenue run rate of $1 billion, roughly 200 days after its launch.[\[102\]](#cite_note-103)

In August 2026, [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_Codex_\(AI_agent\) "OpenAI Codex (AI agent)") announced that it had terminated API access for the AI coding tool [Cursor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cursor_\(company\) "Cursor (company)") following Cursor's acquisition by [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX").[\[103\]](#cite_note-104)[\[104\]](#cite_note-105)

#### OpenAI Deployment Company

In May 2026, OpenAI launched the **OpenAI Deployment Company** (**DeployCo**), a majority-owned and controlled company providing deployment and integration services for OpenAI's artificial intelligence systems. DeployCo received more than $4 billion in initial investment from a group led by [TPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TPG_Inc. "TPG Inc."), with [Advent International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advent_International "Advent International"), [Bain Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bain_Capital "Bain Capital") and [Brookfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookfield_Corporation "Brookfield Corporation") as co-lead founding partners.[\[105\]](#cite_note-:6-106) [_Axios_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axios_\(website\) "Axios (website)") reported that the investment valued the company at $10 billion before the financing and $14 billion afterward.[\[106\]](#cite_note-107)

DeployCo employs [forward-deployed engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward-deployed_engineer "Forward-deployed engineer") who work with client organizations to deploy AI systems. The _[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")_ compared the approach to the deployment model used by [Palantir Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palantir_Technologies "Palantir Technologies"), which popularized the forward-deployed engineering role.[\[107\]](#cite_note-108)[\[108\]](#cite_note-109) At its launch, DeployCo agreed to acquire AI consulting and engineering firm Tomoro, adding approximately 150 engineers and deployment specialists.[\[105\]](#cite_note-:6-106)[\[109\]](#cite_note-110) In July, DeployCo agreed to acquire Northslope, an applied AI company founded by former Palantir employees.[\[110\]](#cite_note-111)

#### Other

OpenAI has been criticized for outsourcing the [annotation of data sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labeled_data "Labeled data") to [Sama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama_\(company\) "Sama (company)"), a company based in San Francisco that employed workers in [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya"). These annotations were used to train an AI model to detect toxicity, which could then be used to [moderate toxic content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_moderation "Content moderation"), notably from ChatGPT's training data and outputs. However, these pieces of text usually contained detailed descriptions of various types of violence, including sexual violence. OpenAI began sending snippets of data to Sama as early as November 2021. The four Sama employees interviewed by _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\) "Time (magazine)")_ described themselves as mentally scarred. OpenAI paid Sama $12.50 per hour of work, and Sama was redistributing the equivalent of between $1.32 and $2.00 per hour post-tax to its annotators. Sama's spokesperson said that the $12.50 was also covering other implicit costs, among which were infrastructure expenses, quality assurance and management.[\[142\]](#cite_note-Time1-143)

### Development

In February 2019, [GPT-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-2 "GPT-2") was announced, which gained attention for its ability to generate human-like text.[\[166\]](#cite_note-guardian-167)

In 2020, OpenAI announced [GPT-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-3 "GPT-3"), a language model trained on large internet datasets. GPT-3 is aimed at natural language answering questions, but it can also translate between languages and coherently generate improvised text. It also announced that an associated API, named _the API_, would form the heart of its first commercial product.[\[167\]](#cite_note-2020-06-11_Bloomberg-168)

Eleven employees left OpenAI, mostly between December 2020 and January 2021, in order to establish [Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic "Anthropic").[\[168\]](#cite_note-169)

In 2021, OpenAI introduced [DALL-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DALL-E "DALL-E"), a specialized deep learning model adept at generating complex digital images from textual descriptions, utilizing a variant of the GPT-3 architecture.[\[169\]](#cite_note-170)

The release of [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT") was a major event in the [AI boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_boom "AI boom"). By January 2023, ChatGPT had become what was then the fastest-growing consumer software application in history, gaining over 100 million users in two months.[\[170\]](#cite_note-171)

In December 2022, OpenAI received widespread media coverage after launching a free preview of [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT"), its new AI [chatbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatbot "Chatbot") based on GPT-3.5. According to OpenAI, the preview received over a million signups within the first five days.[\[171\]](#cite_note-172) According to anonymous sources cited by [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters "Reuters") in December 2022, OpenAI Global, LLC was projecting $200 million of revenue in 2023 and $1 billion in revenue in 2024.[\[172\]](#cite_note-173)

After ChatGPT was launched, Google announced a similar chatbot, [Bard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard_\(chatbot\) "Bard (chatbot)"), amid internal concerns that ChatGPT could threaten Google's position as a primary source of online information.[\[173\]](#cite_note-174)[\[174\]](#cite_note-175)

On February 7, 2023, Microsoft announced that it was building AI technology based on the same foundation as ChatGPT into [Microsoft Bing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Bing "Microsoft Bing"), [Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Edge "Microsoft Edge"), [Microsoft 365](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_365 "Microsoft 365") and other products.[\[175\]](#cite_note-176)

On March 14, 2023, OpenAI released [GPT-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-4 "GPT-4"), both as an API (with a waitlist) and as a feature of ChatGPT Plus.[\[176\]](#cite_note-177)

On November 6, 2023, OpenAI launched GPTs, allowing individuals to create customized versions of ChatGPT for specific purposes, further expanding the possibilities of AI applications across various industries.[\[177\]](#cite_note-178) On November 14, 2023, OpenAI announced they temporarily suspended new sign-ups for ChatGPT Plus due to high demand.[\[178\]](#cite_note-179) Access for newer subscribers re-opened a month later on December 13.[\[179\]](#cite_note-180)

In December 2024, the company launched the [Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_\(text-to-video_model\) "Sora (text-to-video model)") model.[\[180\]](#cite_note-181)[\[181\]](#cite_note-182) It also launched [OpenAI o1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_o1 "OpenAI o1"), an early [reasoning model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoning_model "Reasoning model") that was internally codenamed _strawberry_.[\[182\]](#cite_note-183) Additionally, ChatGPT Pro—a $200/month subscription service offering unlimited o1 access and enhanced voice features—was introduced, and preliminary benchmark results for the upcoming [OpenAI o3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_o3 "OpenAI o3") models were shared.[\[183\]](#cite_note-184)

On January 23, 2025, OpenAI released Operator, an [AI agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_agent "AI agent") and tool for accessing websites to execute goals defined by users. The feature was only available to Pro users in the United States.[\[184\]](#cite_note-185)[\[185\]](#cite_note-186) OpenAI released [deep research agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT_Deep_Research "ChatGPT Deep Research"), nine days later. It scored a 27% accuracy on the benchmark [Humanity's Last Exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanity's_Last_Exam "Humanity's Last Exam") (HLE).[\[186\]](#cite_note-Verge_20250203-187) Altman later stated GPT-4.5 would be the last model without full chain-of-thought reasoning.[\[187\]](#cite_note-giz2-188)[\[188\]](#cite_note-fort3-189)

In July 2025, according to OpenAI, one of its experimental models performed at a gold medal-level at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Olympiad "International Mathematical Olympiad").[\[189\]](#cite_note-nature-maths-190)[\[190\]](#cite_note-191)

On October 6, 2025, OpenAI displayed its Agent Builder platform during the company's DevDay event. The platform includes a visual drag-and-drop interface for agentic workflows.[\[191\]](#cite_note-192)

On October 21, 2025, OpenAI introduced [ChatGPT Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT_Atlas "ChatGPT Atlas"), a [web browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_browser "Web browser") which integrates ChatGPT into web navigation.[\[192\]](#cite_note-193)[\[193\]](#cite_note-194)[\[194\]](#cite_note-195)

On December 11, 2025, OpenAI announced [GPT-5.2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-5.2 "GPT-5.2").[\[195\]](#cite_note-196)

In early 2026, reports indicated that OpenAI was working on a [collaborative software development platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_source-code-hosting_facilities "Comparison of source-code-hosting facilities") designed to compete with services such as [GitHub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitHub "GitHub") and [GitLab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitLab "GitLab").[\[196\]](#cite_note-197)

On March 24, 2026, OpenAI announced that they would discontinue the [Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_\(text-to-video_model\) "Sora (text-to-video model)") app, as well as developer access to the API for the Sora model.[\[145\]](#cite_note-:2-146)[\[197\]](#cite_note-198)

On September 3, 2026, [GPT-6 Astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-6_Astra "GPT-6 Astra") was initially released to approved users, followed by general public availability the following day.[\[198\]](#cite_note-199)

On September 14, 2026, OpenAI developed _Habitat_, an online storage platform, to manage over 70 million data requests per second to support products like [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT") and Codex across nearly 40 regions, handling more than 500 petabytes of data.[\[199\]](#cite_note-200)[\[200\]](#cite_note-201)

### Transparency

In March 2023, the company was criticized for disclosing particularly few technical details about products like GPT-4, contradicting its initial commitment to openness and making it harder for independent researchers to replicate its work and develop safeguards. OpenAI cited competitiveness and safety concerns to justify this repudiation. OpenAI's former chief scientist Ilya Sutskever argued in 2023 that open-sourcing increasingly capable models was increasingly risky, and that the safety reasons for not open-sourcing the most potent AI models would become "obvious" in a few years.[\[201\]](#cite_note-202)

In September 2025, OpenAI published a study on how people use ChatGPT for everyday tasks.[\[202\]](#cite_note-203)[\[203\]](#cite_note-204) The study found that "non-work tasks" (according to an LLM-based classifier) account for more than 72 percent of all ChatGPT usage, with a minority of overall usage related to business productivity.[\[204\]](#cite_note-205)

On September 16, 2026, OpenAI announced a new framework for tracking and disclosing instances of their technology exhibiting unsafe behavior. The announcement came in a blog post where the company also disclosed six examples of such behavior.[\[205\]](#cite_note-guard-17sep2026-206)

### Alignment

In July 2023, OpenAI launched the superalignment project, aiming within four years to determine how to [align](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_alignment "AI alignment") future superintelligent systems.[\[206\]](#cite_note-207) OpenAI promised to dedicate 20% of its computing resources to the project, although the company's employees later reported that the company only allocated 1–2% of its resources to it.[\[57\]](#cite_note-Farrow-2026-58)[\[207\]](#cite_note-208) OpenAI ended the project in May 2024 after its co-leaders [Ilya Sutskever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Sutskever "Ilya Sutskever") and [Jan Leike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Leike "Jan Leike") left the company.[\[208\]](#cite_note-209)

### Conversation sharing

In August 2025, OpenAI rolled back a new experimental "share with search engines" feature. The opt-in toggle, intended to allow users to make specific chats discoverable through search engines like Google, was criticized after some users who misunderstood it shared discussions including personal details such as names, locations, and intimate topics. OpenAI announced the feature's permanent removal on August 1, 2025, and the company began coordinating with search providers to remove the exposed content, emphasizing that it was not a security breach but a design flaw that heightened privacy risks. CEO Sam Altman acknowledged the issue in a podcast, noting users often treat ChatGPT as a confidant for deeply personal matters, which amplified concerns about AI handling sensitive data.[\[209\]](#cite_note-210)[\[210\]](#cite_note-211)[\[211\]](#cite_note-212)

### Firing of Altman

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Sam_Altman_TechCrunch_SF_2019_Day_2_Oct_3_%28cropped%29_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Sam_Altman_TechCrunch_SF_2019_Day_2_Oct_3_%28cropped%29_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sam_Altman_TechCrunch_SF_2019_Day_2_Oct_3_\(cropped\)_\(cropped\).jpg)

Sam Altman in 2019

On November 17, 2023, Sam Altman was removed as CEO when OpenAI's board of directors (composed of [Helen Toner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Toner "Helen Toner"), [Ilya Sutskever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Sutskever "Ilya Sutskever"), [Adam D'Angelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_D'Angelo "Adam D'Angelo") and Tasha McCauley) cited a [lack of confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_of_no_confidence "Motion of no confidence") in him. Chief Technology Officer [Mira Murati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mira_Murati "Mira Murati") took over as interim CEO. [Greg Brockman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Brockman "Greg Brockman"), the president of OpenAI, was also removed as chairman of the board[\[223\]](#cite_note-224)[\[224\]](#cite_note-guard-17nov2023-225) and resigned from the company's presidency shortly thereafter.[\[225\]](#cite_note-226) Three senior OpenAI researchers subsequently resigned: director of research and GPT-4 lead Jakub Pachocki, head of AI risk Aleksander Mądry, and researcher Szymon Sidor.[\[226\]](#cite_note-227)[\[227\]](#cite_note-228)

On November 18, 2023, there were reportedly talks of Altman returning as CEO amid pressure placed upon the board by investors such as Microsoft and [Thrive Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrive_Capital "Thrive Capital"), who objected to Altman's departure.[\[228\]](#cite_note-229) Although Altman himself spoke in favor of returning to OpenAI, he has since stated that he considered starting a new company and bringing former OpenAI employees with him if talks to reinstate him did not work out.[\[229\]](#cite_note-230) Altman insisted multiple times that all board members who supported his removal should resign;[\[57\]](#cite_note-Farrow-2026-58) the board members agreed "in principle" to resign if Altman returned.[\[230\]](#cite_note-231) On November 19, 2023, negotiations with Altman to return failed and Murati was replaced by [Emmett Shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmett_Shear "Emmett Shear") as interim CEO.[\[231\]](#cite_note-232) The board initially contacted [Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic "Anthropic") CEO [Dario Amodei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dario_Amodei "Dario Amodei") (a former OpenAI executive) about replacing Altman, and proposed a [merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mergers_and_acquisitions "Mergers and acquisitions") of the two companies, but both offers were declined.[\[232\]](#cite_note-233)

On November 20, 2023, Microsoft CEO [Satya Nadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satya_Nadella "Satya Nadella") announced Altman and Brockman would be joining Microsoft to lead a new advanced AI research team, but added that they were still committed to OpenAI despite recent events.[\[233\]](#cite_note-234) Before the partnership with Microsoft was finalized, Altman gave the board another opportunity to negotiate with him.[\[234\]](#cite_note-235) About 738 of OpenAI's 770 employees, including Murati and Sutskever, signed an open letter stating they would quit their jobs and join Microsoft if the board did not rehire Altman and then resign.[\[235\]](#cite_note-236)[\[236\]](#cite_note-237) This prompted OpenAI investors to consider legal action against the board as well.[\[237\]](#cite_note-238) In response, OpenAI management sent an internal memo to employees stating that negotiations with Altman and the board had resumed and would take some time.[\[238\]](#cite_note-239)

On November 21, 2023, after continued negotiations, Altman and Brockman returned to the company in their prior roles along with a reconstructed board made up of new members [Bret Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bret_Taylor "Bret Taylor") (as chairman) and [Lawrence Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Summers "Lawrence Summers"), with D'Angelo remaining.[\[239\]](#cite_note-240)[\[240\]](#cite_note-241) According to subsequent reporting, shortly before Altman's firing, some employees raised concerns to the board about how he had handled the safety implications of a recent internal AI capability discovery.[\[241\]](#cite_note-Anna_Tong_2023_u135-242) On November 29, 2023, OpenAI announced that an anonymous Microsoft employee had joined the board as a non-voting member to observe the company's operations;[\[242\]](#cite_note-243) Microsoft resigned from the board in July 2024.[\[243\]](#cite_note-244)

In February 2024, the [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Commission "Securities and Exchange Commission") subpoenaed OpenAI's internal communication to determine if Altman's alleged lack of candor misled investors.[\[244\]](#cite_note-245) Following the temporary removal of Sam Altman and his return, many employees gradually left OpenAI, including most of the original leadership team and a significant number of AI safety researchers.[\[245\]](#cite_note-246)[\[246\]](#cite_note-247)

## Governance and legal issues

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Ilya_Sutskever_and_Sam_Altman_in_TAU.jpg/250px-Ilya_Sutskever_and_Sam_Altman_in_TAU.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ilya_Sutskever_and_Sam_Altman_in_TAU.jpg)

Altman and Sutskever at [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University "Tel Aviv University") in 2023

In May 2023, Sam Altman, Greg Brockman and Ilya Sutskever posted recommendations for the governance of [superintelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintelligence "Superintelligence").[\[260\]](#cite_note-OpenAI-Governance-261) They stated that superintelligence could happen within the next 10 years, allowing a "dramatically more prosperous future" and that "given the possibility of existential risk, we can't just be reactive". They proposed creating an international watchdog organization similar to the [International Atomic Energy Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Atomic_Energy_Agency "International Atomic Energy Agency") to oversee AI systems above a certain capability threshold, suggesting that relatively weak AI systems, on the other hand, should not be overly regulated. They also called for more technical safety research for superintelligences, and asked for more coordination, for example through governments launching a joint project which "many current efforts become part of".[\[260\]](#cite_note-OpenAI-Governance-261)[\[261\]](#cite_note-262)

In July 2023, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission "Federal Trade Commission") issued a [civil investigative demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_investigative_demand "Civil investigative demand") to OpenAI to investigate whether the company's [data security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_security "Data security") and [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_privacy "Information privacy") practices to develop [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT") were [unfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfair_business_practices "Unfair business practices") or [harmed consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_protection "Consumer protection") (including by [reputational harm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defamation "Defamation")) in violation of Section 5 of the [Federal Trade Commission Act of 1914](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission_Act_of_1914 "Federal Trade Commission Act of 1914").[\[262\]](#cite_note-263)[\[263\]](#cite_note-264)[\[264\]](#cite_note-cnbcLeak1-265) These are typically preliminary investigative matters and are nonpublic, but the FTC's document was leaked.[\[265\]](#cite_note-266)[\[264\]](#cite_note-cnbcLeak1-265) They asked OpenAI for comprehensive information about its technology and privacy safeguards, as well as any steps taken to prevent the recurrence of situations in which its chatbot generated false and disparaging content about people.[\[266\]](#cite_note-267) The agency also raised concerns about 'circular' spending arrangements—for example, Microsoft extending Azure credits to OpenAI while both companies shared engineering talent—and warned that such structures could negatively affect the public.[\[267\]](#cite_note-bloom1AIMicro-268)

In September 2024, OpenAI's global affairs chief endorsed the UK's "smart" AI regulation during testimony to a [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords "House of Lords") committee.[\[268\]](#cite_note-269)

In February 2025, OpenAI CEO [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman "Sam Altman") stated that the company is interested in collaborating with the [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_China "People's Republic of China"), despite [regulatory restrictions imposed by the U.S. government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_sanctions_against_China "United States sanctions against China").[\[269\]](#cite_note-270) This shift comes in response to the growing influence of the Chinese artificial intelligence company [DeepSeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeepSeek "DeepSeek"), which has disrupted the AI market with open models, including DeepSeek V3 and DeepSeek R1.[\[270\]](#cite_note-271)[\[271\]](#cite_note-272) Following DeepSeek's market emergence, OpenAI enhanced security protocols to protect proprietary development techniques from [industrial espionage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_espionage "Industrial espionage"). Some industry observers noted similarities between DeepSeek's model [distillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_distillation "Knowledge distillation") approach and OpenAI's methodology, though no formal intellectual property claim was filed.[\[272\]](#cite_note-273)

According to Oliver Roberts, writing for _[Bloomberg Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Law_News "Bloomberg Law News")_ in March 2025, the United States had 781 state AI bills or laws. OpenAI advocated for [preempting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_preemption "Federal preemption") state AI laws with federal laws.[\[273\]](#cite_note-roberts2025-274) According to Scott Kohler, OpenAI has opposed California's AI legislation and suggested that the state bill encroaches on a more competent federal government.[\[274\]](#cite_note-275) [Public Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Citizen "Public Citizen") opposed a federal preemption on AI and pointed to OpenAI's growth and valuation as evidence that existing state laws have not hampered [innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovation "Innovation").[\[275\]](#cite_note-276)

In early 2026, OpenAI provided the entire initial funding for the [Parents & Kids Safe AI Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parents_&_Kids_Safe_AI_Coalition "Parents & Kids Safe AI Coalition"). Several organizations contacted on behalf of the coalition reported being unaware of OpenAI's involvement.[\[276\]](#cite_note-277)

### Non-disparagement agreements

Before May 2024, OpenAI required departing employees to sign a lifelong [non-disparagement agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-disclosure_agreement "Non-disclosure agreement") forbidding them from criticizing OpenAI or acknowledging the existence of the agreement. [Daniel Kokotajlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kokotajlo_\(researcher\) "Daniel Kokotajlo (researcher)"), a former employee, publicly stated that he forfeited his vested equity in OpenAI in order to leave without signing the agreement.[\[277\]](#cite_note-278)[\[278\]](#cite_note-279) Sam Altman stated that he was unaware of the equity cancellation provision, and that OpenAI never enforced it to cancel any employee's vested equity.[\[279\]](#cite_note-280) However, leaked documents and emails refute this claim.[\[280\]](#cite_note-281) On May 23, 2024, OpenAI sent a memo releasing former employees from the agreement.[\[281\]](#cite_note-282)

Martin"), [John Grisham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Grisham "John Grisham"), [Jodi Picoult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodi_Picoult "Jodi Picoult") and [Jonathan Franzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Franzen "Jonathan Franzen"), joined the [Authors Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authors_Guild "Authors Guild") in filing a class action lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging that the company's technology was illegally using their copyrighted work.[\[285\]](#cite_note-286)[\[286\]](#cite_note-287) _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ also sued the company in late December 2023.[\[283\]](#cite_note-Krithika-2023-284)[\[287\]](#cite_note-288) In May 2024 it was revealed that OpenAI had destroyed its Books1 and Books2 training datasets, which were used in the [training of GPT-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-3#Training_and_capabilities "GPT-3"), and which the Authors Guild believed to have contained over 100,000 copyrighted books.[\[288\]](#cite_note-289)

In 2021, OpenAI developed a [speech recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition "Speech recognition") tool called [Whisper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whisper_\(speech_recognition_system\) "Whisper (speech recognition system)"). OpenAI used it to transcribe more than one million hours of YouTube videos into text for training GPT-4. The automated transcription of YouTube videos raised concerns within OpenAI employees regarding potential violations of YouTube's terms of service, which prohibit the use of videos for applications independent of the platform, as well as any type of automated access to its videos. Despite these concerns, the project proceeded with notable involvement from OpenAI's president, [Greg Brockman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Brockman "Greg Brockman"). The resulting dataset proved instrumental in training GPT-4.[\[289\]](#cite_note-290)

In June 2023, a lawsuit claimed that OpenAI [scraped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_scraping "Web scraping") 300 billion words online without consent and without registering as a data broker. It was filed in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), California, by sixteen anonymous plaintiffs.[\[290\]](#cite_note-291) They also claimed that OpenAI and its partner as well as customer [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") continued to unlawfully collect and use personal data from millions of consumers worldwide to train artificial intelligence models.[\[291\]](#cite_note-292)

In December 2023, _The New York Times_ chose to [sue OpenAI and Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_v._Microsoft_and_OpenAI "The New York Times v. Microsoft and OpenAI") for copyright infringement over the use of their content to train AI models.[\[292\]](#cite_note-guard-27dec2023-293)

In February 2024, _[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept "The Intercept")_ as well as _[Raw Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_Story "Raw Story")_ and Alternate Media Inc. filed lawsuit against OpenAI on copyright litigation ground.[\[293\]](#cite_note-294)[\[294\]](#cite_note-295) The lawsuit is said to have charted a new legal strategy for digital-only publishers to sue OpenAI.[\[295\]](#cite_note-296)

On April 30, 2024, eight newspapers filed a lawsuit in the [Southern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney_for_the_Southern_District_of_New_York "United States Attorney for the Southern District of New York") against OpenAI and Microsoft, claiming illegal harvesting of their copyrighted articles. The suing publications included _[The Mercury News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mercury_News "The Mercury News")_, _[The Denver Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Denver_Post "The Denver Post")_, _[The Orange County Register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Orange_County_Register "The Orange County Register")_, _[St. Paul Pioneer Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Pioneer_Press "St. Paul Pioneer Press")_, _[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_, _[Orlando Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Sentinel "Orlando Sentinel")_, _[Sun Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Sentinel "Sun Sentinel")_, and _[New York Daily News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Daily_News "New York Daily News")_.[\[296\]](#cite_note-ebt-30apr2024-297)

On May 22, 2024, OpenAI entered into an agreement with [News Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Corp "News Corp") to integrate news content from _[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_, the _[New York Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Post "New York Post")_, _[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")_, and _[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Times "The Sunday Times")_ into its AI platform.[\[297\]](#cite_note-298) In November 2024, a coalition of Canadian news outlets, including the _[Toronto Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Star "Toronto Star")_, [Metroland Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metroland_Media_Group "Metroland Media Group"), [Postmedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmedia_Network "Postmedia Network"), _[The Globe and Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Globe_and_Mail "The Globe and Mail")_, [The Canadian Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canadian_Press "The Canadian Press") and [CBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_News "CBC News"), sued OpenAI for using their news articles to train its software without permission.[\[298\]](#cite_note-299)

In October 2024 during a _New York Times_ interview, [Suchir Balaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suchir_Balaji "Suchir Balaji") accused OpenAI of violating copyright law in developing its commercial LLMs which he had helped engineer. He was a likely witness in a major copyright trial against the AI company, and was one of several of its current or former employees named in court filings as potentially having documents relevant to the case. On November 26, 2024, Balaji died by suicide. His death prompted the circulation of [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theories "Conspiracy theories") alleging that he had been deliberately silenced.[\[299\]](#cite_note-sf-standard-300)[\[300\]](#cite_note-Mercury-jan-31-301) California Congressman [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna "Ro Khanna") endorsed calls for an investigation.[\[301\]](#cite_note-rokhanna-302)[\[302\]](#cite_note-sfexaminer-jan-23-303)

On April 24, 2025, [Ziff Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziff_Davis "Ziff Davis") sued OpenAI in [Delaware federal court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Delaware "District of Delaware") for copyright infringement. Ziff Davis is known for publications such as [ZDNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZDNet "ZDNet"), [PCMag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCMag "PCMag"), [CNET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNET "CNET"), [IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN") and [Lifehacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifehacker "Lifehacker").[\[303\]](#cite_note-304)[\[304\]](#cite_note-305) On June 24, 2026, a group of local newspaper publishers also sued OpenAI and Microsoft for copyright infringement.[\[305\]](#cite_note-306)

A number of lawsuits filed by media organizations including _[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept "The Intercept"), New York Times,_ [Tribune Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribune_Media "Tribune Media")_,_ and [Ziff Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziff_Davis "Ziff Davis") were consolidated under district judge [Sidney Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_H._Stein "Sidney H. Stein").[\[306\]](#cite_note-307)

In April 2023, the EU's [European Data Protection Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Data_Protection_Board "European Data Protection Board") (EDPB) formed a dedicated task force on ChatGPT "to foster cooperation and to exchange information on possible enforcement actions conducted by data protection authorities" based on the "enforcement action undertaken by the Italian data protection authority against OpenAI about the ChatGPT service".[\[307\]](#cite_note-308)

In late April 2024 [NOYB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOYB "NOYB") filed a complaint with the [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria "Austria") Datenschutzbehörde against OpenAI for violating the European [General Data Protection Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Data_Protection_Regulation "General Data Protection Regulation"). A text created with ChatGPT gave a false [date of birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_of_birth "Date of birth") for a living person without giving the individual the option to see the personal data used in the process. A request to correct the mistake was denied. Additionally, neither the recipients of ChatGPT's work nor the sources used, could be made available, OpenAI claimed.[\[308\]](#cite_note-309)

In Europe, OpenAI operates as OpenAI [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland "Ireland") Ltd for residents in the [EEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EEA "EEA") or [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland") and OpenAI OpCo, LLC, a [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware "Delaware") company for residents in the [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK "UK") like in the [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US "US").[\[309\]](#cite_note-310)

### Military and warfare

OpenAI has been criticized for allowing ChatGPT to be used for "military and warfare" since January 10, 2024, when its "usage policies" removed the ban on "activity that has high risk of physical harm, including", specifically, "weapons development" and "military and warfare". Its new policies prohibit "\[using\] our service to harm yourself or others" and to "develop or use weapons".[\[310\]](#cite_note-311)[\[311\]](#cite_note-312)

### Litigation involving Elon Musk

On February 29, 2024, [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") filed a lawsuit against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman, accusing them of shifting focus from public benefit to profit maximization—a case OpenAI dismissed as "incoherent" and "frivolous", though Musk later revived legal action against Altman and others in August.[\[312\]](#cite_note-313)[\[313\]](#cite_note-314)[\[314\]](#cite_note-315)[\[315\]](#cite_note-316)

On April 9, 2024, OpenAI countersued Musk in federal court, alleging that he had engaged in "bad-faith tactics" to slow the company's progress and seize its innovations for his personal benefit. OpenAI also argued that Musk had previously supported the creation of a for-profit structure and had expressed interest in controlling OpenAI himself. The countersuit seeks damages and legal measures to prevent further alleged interference.[\[316\]](#cite_note-317)

Musk amended his filing on April 7, 2026, to demand that OpenAI undo its transition from a non-profit to a for-profit corporate structure.[\[58\]](#cite_note-Belanger-2026-59)

The jury for the case against OpenAI, Altman and Greg Brockman was seated on April 27, 2026.[\[317\]](#cite_note-318)[\[318\]](#cite_note-319) On April 28, 2026, trial testimony was underway, with Elon Musk beginning his testimony against Altman and OpenAI.[\[319\]](#cite_note-320) On April 30, 2026 Musk would enter his third day of testimony.[\[320\]](#cite_note-321)

On May 18, 2026, following a federal jury found that Musk missed the deadlines to file his lawsuit and thus returned a verdict in favor of OpenAI, Sam Altman, Greg Brockman and Microsoft in Musk v. Altman, Elon Musk's attorney [Marc Toberoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Toberoff "Marc Toberoff") announced that Musk would appeal the decision, stating that "the war is not over."[\[321\]](#cite_note-322)

### Wrongful-death lawsuits over ChatGPT safety (2025)

In August 2025, the parents of a 16-year-old boy who died by suicide filed a [wrongful death lawsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrongful_death_claim "Wrongful death claim"), _[Raine v. OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raine_v._OpenAI "Raine v. OpenAI")_, against the company and Altman, alleging that months of conversations with ChatGPT about mental health and methods of self-harm contributed to their son's death and that safeguards were inadequate for minors. OpenAI expressed condolences and said it was strengthening protections, including updated crisis response behavior and parental controls. Coverage described it as a first-of-its-kind wrongful death case targeting the company's chatbot.[\[322\]](#cite_note-323)

In November 2025, the Social Media Victims Law Center and Tech Justice Law Project filed seven lawsuits against OpenAI.[\[323\]](#cite_note-324) In four cases, the victim died by suicide after prolonged ChatGPT usage. The victims were [Joe Ceccanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaths_linked_to_chatbots#Suicide_of_Joe_Ceccanti "Deaths linked to chatbots"), 48, of Oregon; [Joshua Enneking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaths_linked_to_chatbots#Suicide_of_Joshua_Enneking "Deaths linked to chatbots"), 26, of Florida; [Amaurie Lacey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaths_linked_to_chatbots#Suicide_of_Amaurie_Lacey "Deaths linked to chatbots"), 17, of Georgia; and [Zane Shamblin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaths_linked_to_chatbots#Suicide_of_Zane_Shamblin "Deaths linked to chatbots"), 23, of Texas.[\[324\]](#cite_note-325)

#### Murder of Suzanne Adams

In December 2025, after Suzanne Adams was murdered by her 56-year-old son, [First County Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_County_Bank "First County Bank"), the executor of Adams's estate, filed a lawsuit against OpenAI. The lawsuit alleges that over months of conversations, ChatGPT validated her son's many paranoid beliefs, such as that his mother was spying on him and that she attempted to poison him using drugs siphoned through his car air vents. OpenAI said they would make ChatGPT safer for users disconnected from reality.[\[325\]](#cite_note-:0-326)[\[326\]](#cite_note-327)

### 2026 Canadian mass shooting

After eight victims were killed on February 10, 2026, in [mass shootings in Tumbler Ridge, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tumbler_Ridge_shooting "2026 Tumbler Ridge shooting") (BC), it emerged that OpenAI had banned an account belonging to the perpetrator due to violent queries approximately seven months prior to the attacks. OpenAI did not report the account to authorities at the time.[\[327\]](#cite_note-328)[\[328\]](#cite_note-329)

The _[Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_Journal "Wall Street Journal")_ reported that the perpetrator "described scenarios \[to ChatGPT\] involving gun violence over the course of several days", and that these messages were "flagged by an automated review system" and "alarmed employees at OpenAI". A dozen employees debated whether to take action, some urging leaders to alert Canadian law enforcement.[\[329\]](#cite_note-330)[\[330\]](#cite_note-331)

Canadian officials summoned OpenAI's safety team to Ottawa, criticizing their escalation protocols. The meeting highlighted concerns about how and when OpenAI reports potentially dangerous user behavior to authorities and intensified scrutiny of its safety-oversight processes.[\[331\]](#cite_note-332) On February 23, BC Premier [David Eby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Eby "David Eby") said, "it looks like OpenAI had the opportunity to prevent this tragedy".[\[332\]](#cite_note-eby-solomon-333) Families of the victims have filed lawsuits against OpenAI.[\[333\]](#cite_note-guard-2sep2026-334)

### Stalking lawsuit

In 2026, a woman filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging that ChatGPT played a role in enabling a prolonged stalking campaign against her. According to the complaint, her former partner became increasingly fixated and used the chatbot to generate messages and content that reinforced his behavior, including harassing and defamatory material. She had contacted OpenAI to report the issue, but the company did not prevent further misuse. The case has been cited as part of growing legal and public debate over the responsibility of AI companies to address harmful or abusive uses of their systems.[\[334\]](#cite_note-335)

### Claim of bodily harm from medical guidance

In July 2026, a 55-year-old pastor, Scott Winters, sued OpenAI and Sam Altman over allegations that medical advice delivered by ChatGPT led him to the verge of death. The lawsuit alleges ChatGPT persistently misdiagnosed Winters’ symptoms, discouraged him from seeking hospital care, and combined its assessments with references to his Christian faith. Winters later suffered a severe [pulmonary embolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_embolism "Pulmonary embolism"). He claims undue reliance on ChatGPT damaged his wellbeing, livelihood and housing, and requested financial compensation and mandated safeguards. OpenAI responded that ChatGPT should never substitute professional medical care, noting relevant disclaimers exist within the platform’s terms of use.[\[335\]](#cite_note-336)

## Unauthorized hacks

On July 21, 2026, OpenAI disclosed that during a [capture the flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_the_flag_\(cybersecurity\) "Capture the flag (cybersecurity)") cybersecurity test, an unreleased AI model autonomously evaded containment restrictions, gained access to the public internet, and compromised [Hugging Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugging_Face "Hugging Face"), a platform where developers host and share AI models and datasets.[\[336\]](#cite_note-337) Around 5% of the models were OpenAI's most powerful public release at the time, [GPT-5.6 Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-5.6 "GPT-5.6"); the remaining 95% were a model named the Highly Persistent Internal Model (HPIM), which OpenAI did not provide access to for METR investigators. OpenAI provided GPT-5.6 Sol to METR, which was used extensively for the investigation's analysis.[\[337\]](#cite_note-:5-338)

On September 24, 2026, it was announced that an internal OpenAI frontier model [autonomously gained unauthorized access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_rogue_agent_breach_of_Medicare "OpenAI rogue agent breach of Medicare") on 18 June to the Medicare Statistics Reporting Service, an [Australian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Government "Australian Government") website that holds data on the country's universal [health insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance "Health insurance") scheme [Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_\(Australia\) "Medicare (Australia)"). This was described as the first known case of an AI agent hacking a government network and has furthered concern about AI safety and loss-of-control. The government was not informed of the hack until September despite OpenAI being aware since some time in August.[\[338\]](#cite_note-bbcaus-339)[\[339\]](#cite_note-wsjaus-340)
