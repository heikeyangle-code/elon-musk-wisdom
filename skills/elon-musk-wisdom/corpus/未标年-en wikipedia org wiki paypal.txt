**PayPal Holdings, Inc.** is an American multinational [financial technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_technology "Financial technology") company operating an online payments system in the majority of countries that support [online money transfers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-commerce_payment_system "E-commerce payment system"); it serves as an electronic alternative to traditional [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banknote "Banknote") methods such as [checks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheque "Cheque") and [money orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_order "Money order"). The company operates as a payment processor for online vendors, [auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction "Auction") sites and many other commercial and company users, which involve a fee.

Established in 1998 as Confinity,[\[4\]](#cite_note-4) PayPal went public through an [IPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") in 2002. It became a wholly owned [subsidiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiary "Subsidiary") of [eBay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBay "EBay") later that year, valued at $1.5 billion.[\[5\]](#cite_note-nytimes02-5) In 2015, eBay [spun off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off "Corporate spin-off") PayPal to its shareholders, and PayPal became an independent company again.[\[2\]](#cite_note-sec_gov-2)[\[6\]](#cite_note-reuters2014-6) The company was ranked 143rd on the 2022 [_Fortune_ 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500 "Fortune 500") of the largest United States corporations by revenue.[\[7\]](#cite_note-Fortune_500_Companies_2018-7)

The company was originally established by [Max Levchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Levchin "Max Levchin"), [Peter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thiel "Peter Thiel"), and [Luke Nosek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Nosek "Luke Nosek") in December 1998 as Fieldlink. Later it was renamed [Confinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confinity "Confinity"),[\[8\]](#cite_note-techrepublic-8) a company which developed security software for hand-held devices.[\[9\]](#cite_note-9) When it had no success with that business model, it switched its focus to a digital wallet.[\[10\]](#cite_note-entrepreneur-10) The first version of the PayPal electronic payments system was launched in 1999.[\[11\]](#cite_note-11)

In March 2000, Confinity merged with [X.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X.com_\(bank\) "X.com (bank)"), an online financial services company founded in March 1999 by [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk"), Harris Fricker, Christopher Payne, and Ed Ho.[\[12\]](#cite_note-fastco2012-12) Musk was optimistic about the future success of the money transfer business Confinity was developing.[\[13\]](#cite_note-Jackson-2006-13) Musk and Bill Harris, then-president and CEO of the combined X.com, disagreed about the potential future success of the money transfer business and Harris left the company in May 2000.[\[14\]](#cite_note-14) In October 2000, Musk decided that X.com would terminate its other internet banking operations and focus on payments.[\[15\]](#cite_note-Startup_Playbook-15) In the same month, Elon Musk was replaced by Peter Thiel as CEO of X.com,[\[16\]](#cite_note-16) which was renamed PayPal in June 2001 and went [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPO "IPO") in 2002.[\[17\]](#cite_note-bizfounders-17)[\[18\]](#cite_note-autogenerated1-18)[\[19\]](#cite_note-19) PayPal's [IPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") listed on [NASDAQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASDAQ "NASDAQ") under the [ticker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticker_symbol "Ticker symbol") PYPL at $13 per share and generated over $61 million.[\[20\]](#cite_note-ecommerce02-20)

### eBay subsidiary (2002–2014)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=PayPal&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: eBay subsidiary (2002–2014)")\]

Shortly after PayPal's IPO, [eBay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBay "EBay") acquired the company on October 3, 2002,[\[21\]](#cite_note-21) for $1.5 billion in eBay stock.[\[15\]](#cite_note-Startup_Playbook-15)[\[22\]](#cite_note-22)[\[23\]](#cite_note-fastcompany2014-23) More than 70 percent of all eBay auctions accepted PayPal payments, and roughly 1 in 4 closed auction listings were transacted via PayPal.[\[24\]](#cite_note-24) PayPal became the default payment method used by the majority of eBay users, and the service competed with eBay's subsidiary [Billpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billpoint "Billpoint"), as well as [Citibank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank "Citibank")'s c2it, [Yahoo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo! "Yahoo!")'s [PayDirect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayDirect "PayDirect"), and [Google Checkout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Checkout "Google Checkout").[\[13\]](#cite_note-Jackson-2006-13)

In 2005, PayPal acquired the [VeriSign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VeriSign "VeriSign") payment solution to provide added security support.[\[25\]](#cite_note-ecommerce05-25) In 2007, PayPal announced a partnership with [MasterCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MasterCard "MasterCard"), which led to the development and launch of the PayPal Secure Card service, a software that allows customers to make payments on websites that do not accept PayPal directly.[\[26\]](#cite_note-reuters2007-26) By the end of 2007, the company generated $1.8 billion in revenue.[\[27\]](#cite_note-cnnmoney-27) In January 2008, PayPal acquired [Fraud Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraud_Sciences "Fraud Sciences"), a privately held Israeli start-up that developed online risk tools, for $169 million.[\[28\]](#cite_note-techcrunchha-28)[\[29\]](#cite_note-29) In November 2008, the company acquired [Bill Me Later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Me_Later "Bill Me Later"), an online transactional credit company.[\[30\]](#cite_note-incmag08-30)

By 2010, PayPal had over 100 million active user accounts in 190 markets through 25 different currencies.[\[31\]](#cite_note-galeecommerce-31) In July 2011, fourteen alleged members of the [Anonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymous_\(group\) "Anonymous (group)") [hacktivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacktivist "Hacktivist") group were charged with attempting to disrupt PayPal's operations. The [denial of service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denial_of_service "Denial of service") attacks occurred in December 2010, after PayPal stopped processing donations to [WikiLeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks "WikiLeaks"). On 5 December 2013, 13 of the [PayPal 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal_14 "PayPal 14") pleaded guilty to misdemeanor and felony charges related to the attacks.[\[32\]](#cite_note-David_Gilbert-32)[\[33\]](#cite_note-Steven_Musil-33)

The company continued to build its Merchant Services division, providing [e-payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-payment "E-payment") for retailers on eBay. In 2011, PayPal announced that it would begin moving its business offline so that customers can make payments via PayPal in stores.[\[34\]](#cite_note-34) In August 2012, the company announced its partnership with [Discover Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discover_Card "Discover Card") to allow PayPal payments to be made at any of the seven million stores in the Discover network.[\[35\]](#cite_note-forbesbarth-35) By the end of 2012, PayPal's total payment volume processed was US$145 billion.[\[36\]](#cite_note-36) and accounted for 40% of eBay's revenue, amounting to US$1.37 billion in the 3rd quarter of 2012.[\[37\]](#cite_note-samuels2012-37)

In 2013, PayPal acquired IronPearl, a [Palo Alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto "Palo Alto") startup offering engagement software,[\[38\]](#cite_note-venturebeat2013-38) and [Braintree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braintree_\(company\) "Braintree (company)"), a [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago")\-based [payment gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payment_gateway "Payment gateway"), to further product development and mobile services.[\[39\]](#cite_note-techcrunchrao-39) In June 2014, [David Marcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_A._Marcus "David A. Marcus") announced he was leaving his role as PayPal President;[\[40\]](#cite_note-40) Marcus joined PayPal in August 2011 after its acquisition of [Zong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zong_mobile_payments "Zong mobile payments"), of which he was the founder and CEO.[\[41\]](#cite_note-duryee2012-41) David Marcus succeeded [Scott Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Thompson_\(businessman\) "Scott Thompson (businessman)") as president, who left the role to join [Yahoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo "Yahoo").[\[41\]](#cite_note-duryee2012-41) PayPal announced that Marcus would be succeeded by [Dan Schulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Schulman "Dan Schulman"), who previously served as CEO of [Virgin Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mobile "Virgin Mobile") and Executive vice president of [American Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Express "American Express").[\[42\]](#cite_note-bizjournal2014-42)

### Spin-off from eBay (2014–present)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=PayPal&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Spin-off from eBay (2014–present)")\]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/PayPal.svg/250px-PayPal.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:PayPal.svg)

Former PayPal logo, used until September 17, 2024

It was announced on September 30, 2014, that eBay would spin off PayPal into a separate publicly traded company, a move demanded in 2013 by activist [hedge fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund "Hedge fund") magnate [Carl Icahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Icahn "Carl Icahn"). The spin-off was completed on July 18, 2015. On July 20, the company was relisted on [Nasdaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasdaq "Nasdaq") under the same ticker symbol it used shortly before it was acquired by eBay, PYPL.[\[43\]](#cite_note-43)[\[44\]](#cite_note-44)[\[45\]](#cite_note-45) On January 31, 2018, eBay announced "After the existing eBay-PayPal agreement ends in 2020, PayPal will remain a payment option for shoppers on eBay, but it won't be prominently featured ahead of debit and credit card options as it is today. PayPal will cease to process card payments for eBay at that time." The company will "instead begin working with [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam "Amsterdam")\-based [Adyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adyen "Adyen")".[\[46\]](#cite_note-46)

On July 1, 2015, PayPal announced that it was acquiring digital money transfer company [Xoom Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xoom_Corporation "Xoom Corporation"). PayPal spent $25 a share in cash to acquire the publicly traded Xoom, or about $1.09 billion. The deal was closed in the fourth quarter of 2015. The move strengthened PayPal’s international business, giving it access to Xoom’s 1.3 million active U.S. customers that spent about $7 billion in the 12 months ending on March 31, to people in 37 countries.[\[47\]](#cite_note-47)[\[48\]](#cite_note-48)

On September 1, 2015, PayPal launched its [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer "Peer-to-peer") payment platform "PayPal.Me", a service that allows users to send a custom link to request funds via text, email, or other messaging platforms.[\[49\]](#cite_note-PayPalMeTechCrunch-49) Custom links are set to be structured as PayPal.me/username/amount requested.[\[49\]](#cite_note-PayPalMeTechCrunch-49) PayPal.Me was launched in 18 countries including the United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Australia, Canada, Russia, Turkey, France, Italy, Spain, Poland, Sweden, Belgium, Norway, Denmark, Netherlands, Austria, and Switzerland.[\[50\]](#cite_note-PayPalMeEngadget-50) PayPal had 170 million users, as of September 2015, and the focus of PayPal.Me was to create a mobile-first user experience that enables faster payment sharing than PayPal's traditional tools.[\[49\]](#cite_note-PayPalMeTechCrunch-49)

On May 17, 2018, PayPal agreed to purchase Swedish payment processor [iZettle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IZettle "IZettle") for $2.2 billion. This was PayPal's largest acquisition until late November 2019 and the company claims that it is the in-store expertise and digital marketing strength that will complement its own online and [mobile payment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_payment "Mobile payment") services.[\[51\]](#cite_note-BBC_News-2018-51) In 2018, PayPal became a jersey patch sponsor of the [Phoenix Suns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Suns "Phoenix Suns").[\[52\]](#cite_note-52) On March 19, 2019, PayPal announced its partnership with [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") as part of the company's new checkout feature, "Checkout on Instagram".[\[53\]](#cite_note-53)[\[54\]](#cite_note-54) In June 2019, PayPal reported that Chief Operating Officer [Bill Ready](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Ready "Bill Ready") would be leaving the company at the end of the year, transitioning into the role of commerce chief for [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google").[\[55\]](#cite_note-55)

In October 2019, PayPal reported a loss of $228 million on investments, largely due to a failed return from a $500 million investment in [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber "Uber").[\[56\]](#cite_note-56)[\[57\]](#cite_note-57) On January 6, 2020, PayPal acquired [Honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey_\(company\) "Honey (company)") for over $4 billion. This is PayPal's largest acquisition to date. It more recently signed a deal with NBCUniversal.[\[58\]](#cite_note-58) In June 2020, PayPal announced a $530 million commitment to support Black-owned businesses and minority communities in the United States.[\[59\]](#cite_note-59)

In January 2021, PayPal became the first foreign operator with 100% control of a payment platform in China, gaining an advanced position in the local online payment market.[\[60\]](#cite_note-60) In an international survey conducted in March 2021 by [Morning Consult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Consult "Morning Consult"), PayPal was found to be the second most trusted brand globally.[\[61\]](#cite_note-61) In June 2022, [Shopify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shopify "Shopify") partnered with PayPal to offer Shopify Payments to merchants in France.[\[62\]](#cite_note-62)[\[63\]](#cite_note-63) In February 2023, PayPal announced layoffs for 2,000 of its workers, or 7% of its total workforce.[\[64\]](#cite_note-64) It was reported in February 2023, that CEO Dan Schulman will step away from his role by the end of 2023. Schulman will continue to serve on the board of directors after vacating the position.[\[65\]](#cite_note-65) In August 2023, the company named [Intuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit "Intuit") executive Alex Chriss CEO, effective September 27, 2023.[\[66\]](#cite_note-66)

In August 2023, PayPal launched a U.S. dollar [stablecoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stablecoin "Stablecoin"), called PayPal USD (PYUSD) for payments and transfers.[\[67\]](#cite_note-67) In November 2023, it was announced that the [SEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission "U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission") launched a legal investigation into both PayPal and [Paxos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paxos_Trust_Company "Paxos Trust Company"), the trust responsible for issuing the stablecoin.[\[68\]](#cite_note-68) PayPal said it was cooperating with the subpoena from the SEC’s Enforcement Division.[\[69\]](#cite_note-69) In October 2023, it was announced PayPal has sold its [reverse logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_logistics "Reverse logistics") subsidiary, [Happy Returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Returns_\(company\) "Happy Returns (company)") to [UPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Parcel_Service "United Parcel Service") for an undisclosed amount.[\[70\]](#cite_note-70)

In 2024, PayPal executives announced the company's aim to revive growth in its branded checkout products amidst increased competition from big tech, following an upward revision of the full-year profit forecast.[\[71\]](#cite_note-71)[\[72\]](#cite_note-72)

In January 2026, PayPal announced the acquisition of Cymbio, an AI-specialized company enabling PayPal to expand into agentic AI.[\[73\]](#cite_note-73)[\[74\]](#cite_note-74) In February 2026, PayPal announced the appointment of [Enrique Lores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Lores "Enrique Lores") as CEO, replacing Alex Chriss.[\[75\]](#cite_note-75)

In February 2026, PayPal Holdings attracted takeover interest from potential buyers after a stock slide wiped out almost half of its value. The shares jumped as much as 9.7 per cent on the news.[\[76\]](#cite_note-76)

In July 2026, it was reported that [Stripe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stripe,_Inc. "Stripe, Inc.") and [Advent International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advent_International "Advent International") had made a joint offer to acquire PayPal. [\[77\]](#cite_note-77)

In addition, total payment volume has increased 29% amounting to $220 billion increasing positive investor sentiment.[\[112\]](#cite_note-112)

PayPal's services allow people to make [financial transactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_transaction "Financial transaction") online by granting the ability to [transfer funds electronically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_funds_transfer "Electronic funds transfer") between individuals and businesses.[\[114\]](#cite_note-howitworks-114) Through PayPal, users can send or receive payments for online auctions on websites like [eBay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBay "EBay"), purchase or sell goods and services, or [donate money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philanthropy "Philanthropy") or receive donations. It is not necessary or required to have a PayPal account to use the company's services.[\[114\]](#cite_note-howitworks-114) Certain packaging may come with [tracking numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracking_number "Tracking number"). PayPal account users can set [currency conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_rate "Exchange rate") option in account settings.[\[115\]](#cite_note-115) The PayPal app is available online or at the [iTunes App Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITunes_App_Store "ITunes App Store") and [Google Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Play "Google Play"). One year after acquiring [Braintree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braintree_\(company\) "Braintree (company)"), PayPal introduced its "One Touch" service, which allows users to pay with a one-touch option on participating merchants websites or apps.[\[116\]](#cite_note-recode2014-116)

In 2007, PayPal acquired the online credit product [Bill Me Later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_me_later "Bill me later"), Inc.,[\[117\]](#cite_note-117) which has since been rebranded as PayPal Credit and provided services for [Comenity Capital Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Data "Alliance Data"), the lender of PayPal Credit accounts. Founded in 2000, Bill Me Later is headquartered in [Timonium, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timonium,_Maryland "Timonium, Maryland").[\[118\]](#cite_note-118) PayPal Credit offers shoppers access to an instant online revolving line of credit at thousands of vendors that accept PayPal, subject to credit approval. PayPal Credit allows consumers to shop online in much the same way as they would with a traditional credit card. The rebranding of Bill Me Later as PayPal Credit also means that consumers can use PayPal Credit to fund transactions virtually anywhere PayPal is accepted.[\[119\]](#cite_note-billmelater.com-119) In 2015 PayPal agreed that PayPal Credit would pay a $25 million fine to settle a complaint filed in Federal Court by the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Financial_Protection_Bureau "Consumer Financial Protection Bureau").[\[120\]](#cite_note-cfpb-120)

From 2009 to 2016, PayPal operated Student Accounts, allowing parents to set up a student account, transfer money into it, and obtain a debit card for student use. The program provided tools to teach how to spend money wisely and take responsibility for actions.[\[121\]](#cite_note-121)[\[122\]](#cite_note-nybits-122) PayPal discontinued Student Accounts in August 2016.

In November 2009, PayPal partially opened its platform, allowing other services to get access to more [APIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/API "API") and to use its infrastructure in order to enable peer-to-peer online transactions.[\[123\]](#cite_note-123) On November 28, 2011, PayPal reported [Black Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Friday_\(shopping\) "Black Friday (shopping)") brought record mobile engagement, including a 538% increase in global mobile payment volume when compared with Black Friday 2010.[\[124\]](#cite_note-post1-124) In 2012, the company launched "PayPal Here", a small business mobile payment system that includes a combination of a free [mobile app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_app "Mobile app") and a small card-reader that plugs into a [smart phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone "Smartphone").[\[125\]](#cite_note-mashable2012-125)

PayPal launched an updated app for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS") and [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\) "Android (operating system)") in 2013 that expanded its mobile app capabilities by allowing users to search for local shops and restaurants that accept PayPal payments, order ahead at participating venues, and access their PayPal Credit accounts (formerly known as Bill Me Later).[\[119\]](#cite_note-billmelater.com-119)

On October 21, 2020, PayPal announced a new service allowing customers to use [cryptocurrencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrencies "Cryptocurrencies") to shop at 26 million merchants on the network starting in 2021. PayPal has been using [Paxos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paxos_Trust_Company "Paxos Trust Company") to provide the back end infrastructure allowing users to manage and trade cryptocurrencies in accordance to [data privacy rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_privacy "Information privacy") and financial regulations. Paxos has been in charge of acquiring the necessary regulatory approvals for PayPal to facilitate cryptocurrency assets. As part of the announcement, PayPal secured the first conditional cryptocurrency license from the [New York State Department of Financial Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Department_of_Financial_Services "New York State Department of Financial Services"), which will allow customers to purchase cryptocurrencies such as [bitcoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin "Bitcoin"), [Litecoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litecoin "Litecoin"), [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum "Ethereum"), and [Bitcoin Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_Cash "Bitcoin Cash").[\[126\]](#cite_note-Reuters_2020-10-21-126)[\[127\]](#cite_note-127)[\[128\]](#cite_note-128) In March 2022, PayPal introduced a flat-fee structure for cryptocurrency transactions under $200; transactions over $200 incur a 1.8% fee for purchases or sales up to $1,000, and 1.5% for any transaction amount greater than $1,000.[\[129\]](#cite_note-129)

As of 2022, PayPal operates in 202 markets and has 426 million active, registered accounts. PayPal allows customers to send, receive, and hold funds in 25 currencies worldwide.[\[130\]](#cite_note-130)\[_[needs update?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\]

### Business model evolution

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=PayPal&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Business model evolution")\]

PayPal's success in users and volumes was the product of a three-phase strategy described by former eBay CEO [Meg Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meg_Whitman "Meg Whitman"): "First, PayPal focused on expanding its service among eBay users in the US. Second, we began expanding PayPal to eBay's international sites. And third, we started to build PayPal's business off eBay."[\[133\]](#cite_note-wallstfolly-133)

In the first phase, payment volumes were coming mostly from the eBay auction website. The system was very attractive to auction sellers, most of which were individuals or small businesses that were unable to accept credit cards, and for consumers as well. In fact, many sellers could not qualify for a credit card Merchant account because they lacked a commercial credit history. The service also appealed to auction buyers because they could fund PayPal accounts using credit cards or bank account balances, without divulging credit card numbers to unknown sellers. PayPal employed an aggressive marketing campaign to accelerate its growth, depositing $10 in new users' PayPal accounts.[\[133\]](#cite_note-wallstfolly-133)

Until 2000, PayPal's strategy was to earn interest on funds in PayPal accounts. However, most recipients of PayPal credits withdrew funds immediately. Also, many senders funded their payments using credit cards, which cost PayPal roughly 2% of payment value per transaction.[\[134\]](#cite_note-134)

To solve this problem, PayPal tailored its product to cater more to business accounts. Instead of relying on interests earned from deposited funds, PayPal started relying on earnings from service charges. They offered seller protection to PayPal account holders, provided that they comply with reimbursement policies. For example, PayPal merchants are either required to retain a traceable proof of shipping to a confirmed address or to provide a signed receipt for items valued over $750.[\[135\]](#cite_note-135)

After fine-tuning PayPal's business model and increasing its domestic and international penetration on eBay, PayPal started its off-eBay strategy. This was based on developing stronger growth in active users by adding users across multiple platforms, despite the slowdown in on-eBay growth and low-single-digit user growth on the eBay site. A late 2003 reorganization created a new business unit within PayPal—Merchant Services—to provide payment solutions to small and large e-commerce merchants outside the eBay auction community. Starting in the second half of 2004, PayPal Merchant Services unveiled several initiatives to enroll online merchants outside the eBay auction community, including:[\[133\]](#cite_note-wallstfolly-133)

*   Lowering its transaction fee for high-volume merchants from 2.2% to 1.9% (while increasing the monthly transaction volume required to qualify for the lowest fee to $100,000)
*   Encouraging its users to recruit non-eBay merchants by increasing its referral bonus to a maximum of $1,000 (versus the previous $100 cap)
*   Persuading credit card gateway providers, including [CyberSource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CyberSource "CyberSource") and Retail Decisions USA, to include PayPal among their offerings to online merchants.
*   Hiring a new sales force to acquire large merchants such as [Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell "Dell"), [Apple's iTunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITunes "ITunes"), and [Yahoo! Stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!_Store "Yahoo! Store"), which hosted thousands of online merchants
*   Reducing fees for online music purchases and other "micropayments"
*   Launching PayPal Mobile, which allowed users to make payments using text messaging on their cell phones

In the United States, PayPal is licensed as a [money transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_transmitter "Money transmitter"), on a state-by-state basis.[\[182\]](#cite_note-Wolverton-182)[\[183\]](#cite_note-183) But state laws vary, as do their definitions of banks, narrow banks, money services businesses, and money transmitters. Although PayPal is not classified as a bank, the company is subject to some of the rules and regulations governing the financial industry, including [Regulation E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_E "Regulation E") consumer protections and the [USA PATRIOT Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_PATRIOT_Act "USA PATRIOT Act").[\[184\]](#cite_note-184)

The most analogous regulatory source of law for PayPal transactions comes from [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer_banking "Peer-to-peer banking") (P2P) payments using [credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_card "Credit card") and [debit cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debit_cards "Debit cards"). Ordinarily, a credit card transaction, specifically the relationship between the issuing bank and the cardholder, is governed by the [Truth in Lending Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_in_Lending_Act "Truth in Lending Act") (TILA) 15 U.S.C. §§ 1601-1667f as implemented by Regulation Z, 12 C.F.R. 226, (TILA/Z). TILA/Z requires specific procedures for billing errors, dispute resolution, and limits cardholder liability for unauthorized charges.[\[185\]](#cite_note-margaretjane-185) Similarly, the legal relationship between a debit cardholder and the issuing bank is regulated by the [Electronic Funds Transfer Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Funds_Transfer_Act "Electronic Funds Transfer Act") (EFTA) 15 U.S.C. §§ 1693-1693r, as implemented by Regulation E, 12 C.F.R. 205, (EFTA/E). EFTA/E is directed at consumer protection and provides strict error resolution procedures. However, because PayPal is a _payment intermediary_ and not otherwise regulated directly, TILA/Z and EFTA/E do not operate exactly as written once the credit/debit card transaction occurs via PayPal. Basically, unless a PayPal transaction is funded with a credit card, the consumer has no recourse in the event of fraud by the seller.[\[185\]](#cite_note-margaretjane-185)

In 2008, PayPal Europe was granted a Luxembourg banking license, which, under European Union (EU) law, allows it to conduct banking business throughout the EU.[\[186\]](#cite_note-186) It is therefore regulated as a bank by Luxembourg's banking supervisory authority, the [Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_de_Surveillance_du_Secteur_Financier "Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier") (CSSF).[\[187\]](#cite_note-187)[\[188\]](#cite_note-188)[\[189\]](#cite_note-189) All of the company's European accounts were transferred to PayPal's bank in Luxembourg in July 2007.[\[190\]](#cite_note-190) Prior to the move, PayPal had been registered in the United Kingdom (UK) as PayPal (Europe) Ltd, an entity which was licensed as an Electronic Money Issuer with the UK's [Financial Services Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Services_Authority "Financial Services Authority") (FSA) from 2004. This ceased in 2007, when the company moved to Luxembourg.[\[191\]](#cite_note-191)

In India, as of January 2010, PayPal has no cross-border money transfer authorization. In an emailed response to questions from [The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times"), Reserve Bank of India spokeswoman Alpana Killawalla said, "Providers of cross-border money transfer service need prior authorization from the Reserve Bank under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act. PayPal does not have our authorization."[\[192\]](#cite_note-192) As of 2010, PayPal did not have the required Certificate of Authorization.[\[193\]](#cite_note-193)

PaisaPay is an Indian sister service to PayPal. It is owned by eBay.[\[194\]](#cite_note-paisapay-194) PaisaPay makes possible payments from abroad by PayPal account holders to Indian sellers on eBay.in.

In Australia, PayPal is licensed as an [authorised deposit-taking institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authorised_deposit-taking_institution "Authorised deposit-taking institution") (ADI) and is thus subject to Australian banking laws and regulations.[\[195\]](#cite_note-195)

In Singapore, PayPal holds a stored value facility that does not require the approval of the [Monetary Authority of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_Authority_of_Singapore "Monetary Authority of Singapore").[\[196\]](#cite_note-196)

## Safety and protection policies

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=PayPal&action=edit&section=32 "Edit section: Safety and protection policies")\]

The PayPal Buyer Protection Policy states that customers may file a buyer complaint for not receiving an item or if the purchased item was significantly not as described. The customer can open a dispute within 180 days from the date of payment and escalate it to a claim within 20 days from opening the dispute.

Buyers using a credit card might get a refund via [chargeback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chargeback "Chargeback") from their credit-card company. However, in the UK, where such a purchaser is entitled to specific statutory protections (that the credit card company is a second party to the purchase and is therefore equally liable in law if the other party defaults or goes into liquidation) under Section 75 Consumer Credit Act 1974, the purchaser loses this legal protection if the card payment is processed via PayPal.[\[197\]](#cite_note-197)

Also, the Financial Ombudsman Service (for the U.K.) position is that section 75 protection does not apply where PayPal or any eMoney service becomes involved in the credit card transaction. This leaves consumers with no recourse to pursue their complaint with the Financial Ombudsman Service. They only would have recourse in the courts, but in any event they cannot because PayPal is incorporated in Luxembourg and, since the UK has left the EU, is now no longer within the jurisdiction of any UK Courts. The key issues that determine the applicability of section 75 are identified very clearly in Office of Fair Trading v Lloyds TSB Bank Plc and others \[2006\] EWCA Civ 268 7 and the [Bank of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Scotland "Bank of Scotland") v Alfred Truman (a firm) \[2005\] \[EWHC\] 583 (QB). This is a legal authority that section 75 protection does exist where one has paid on a credit card for a product, via an eMoney service.[\[198\]](#cite_note-198)

According to PayPal, it protects sellers in a limited fashion via the Seller Protection Policy. In general, the Seller Protection Policy is intended to protect the seller from certain kinds of chargebacks or complaints if the seller meets certain conditions, including proof of delivery to the buyer. PayPal states the Seller Protection Policy is "designed to protect sellers against claims by buyers of unauthorized payments and against claims of non-receipt of any merchandise". The policy includes a list of "Exclusions" which itself includes "Intangible goods", "Claims for receipt of goods 'not as described'", and "Total reversals over the annual limit".[\[199\]](#cite_note-ppsafety-199) There are also other restrictions in terms of the sale itself, the payment method and the destination country the item is shipped to (simply having a tracking mechanism is not sufficient to guarantee the Seller Protection Policy is in effect). The PayPal Seller Protection Policy does not provide the additional consumer protection afforded by UK consumer legislation _(most notably the [Consumer Rights Act 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Rights_Act_2015 "Consumer Rights Act 2015"))_ and in addition, it cannot be enforced in the Courts because PayPal operates from Luxembourg, outside all three of the UK legal jurisdictions.[\[200\]](#cite_note-ppagreement-200)

In early 2006, PayPal introduced an optional [security key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_token "Security token") as an additional precaution against fraud.[\[201\]](#cite_note-forbessecurity-201) A user account tied to a security key has a modified login process. Account-holders enter their login ID and password as normal, but are then prompted to enter a six-digit code provided by a credit card sized hardware security key or a text message sent to the account holder's mobile phone. For convenience, users may append the code generated by the hardware key to their password in the login screen. This way they are not prompted for it on another page. This method is required for some services, such as when using PayPal through the eBay application on [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone").

This [two-factor authentication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-factor_authentication "Two-factor authentication") is intended to make it difficult for an account to be compromised by a malicious third party without access to the physical security key, although it does not prevent the so-called [Man in the Browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_in_the_Browser "Man in the Browser") (MITB) attacks. However, the user (or malicious third party) can alternatively authenticate by providing the credit card or bank account number listed on their account. Thus, the PayPal implementation does not offer the security of true two-factor authentication.[\[202\]](#cite_note-techhivepp-202)

It is also possible to use a mobile phone to receive an [mTAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTAN "MTAN") (Mobile Transaction Authentication Number) via [SMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS "SMS").[\[203\]](#cite_note-203) Use of a security code that is sent to the account holder's mobile phone is currently free.[\[204\]](#cite_note-204)

As early as 2001, PayPal had substantial problems with [online fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_fraud "Online fraud"), especially international [hackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker "Hacker") who were hacking into PayPal accounts and transferring small amounts of money out of multiple accounts. Standard solutions for merchant and banking fraud might use government criminal sanctions to pursue the fraudsters. But with PayPal losing millions of dollars each month to fraud while experiencing difficulties with using the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI") to pursue cases of international fraud, PayPal developed a fraud monitoring system to detect potentially fraudulent transactions.[\[205\]](#cite_note-205) This development of fraud monitoring software at PayPal led Peter Thiel to create [Palantir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palantir_Technologies "Palantir Technologies"), a big-data security company whose original mission was to "reduce terrorism while preserving civil liberties".[\[206\]](#cite_note-206)

### Predatory business practices

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=PayPal&action=edit&section=37 "Edit section: Predatory business practices")\]

On December 21, 2024, YouTuber MegaLag uploaded a video revealing how the popular online shopping web browser extension [Honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal_Honey "PayPal Honey") was indiscriminately stealing money from online creators who use [affiliate links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affiliate_links "Affiliate links"), including from YouTubers who Honey was paying to sponsor the extension in their videos.

Usually, when a person clicks on an affiliate link to a product—such as one provided in the description of a YouTube video sponsoring the product, or one embedded in a product review article—a revenue cookie is generated for the affiliate link, so that if someone buys a product because they saw a promotion of that product, the promotor will be credited for the sale and be paid a percentage. If the buyer clicks on multiple affiliate links for a product before buying it, usually the last clicked link is the one credited for the sale.

However, if a buyer had the Honey extension installed in their browser, then upon reaching a merchant's checkout page, the extension would ask if the buyer wanted Honey to check for coupons. If the buyer clicked yes, Honey would delete the cookie for the last clicked affiliate link and replace it with Honey's own affiliate cookie, regardless of whether or not the buyer used any of the coupons Honey found, and even if Honey didn't offer any coupons at all. By doing this, Honey would take all the affiliate credit and money for the sale, leaving the creator who actually promoted the product (who owned the supplanted affiliate link) with nothing.

Honey has also been exposed for intentionally hiding better deals that users would recommend online and instead pulling out worse discounts from the cashback program, which the company has full control over.[\[210\]](#cite_note-210)[\[211\]](#cite_note-211)[\[212\]](#cite_note-212)

## Criticism and controversies

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=PayPal&action=edit&section=38 "Edit section: Criticism and controversies")\]

In 2003, PayPal voluntarily ceased serving as a payment intermediary between gambling websites and their online customers. At the time of this cessation, it was the largest payment processor for online gambling transactions. In 2010, PayPal resumed accepting such transactions, but only in those countries where online gambling is legal, and only for sites which are properly licensed to operate in those jurisdictions.[\[213\]](#cite_note-213)

Since at least 2005, PayPal has maintained an Acceptable Use policy which disallows "transactions involving... items that are considered obscene... \[or\] certain sexually oriented materials or services". Their enforcement of the policy has been a constant source of controversy between PayPal and people within or related to the sex industry. In 2014, PayPal notified subscription service provider [Patreon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patreon "Patreon") that it was moving to cease integration with Patreon as a platform as the result of Patreon permitting "adult content" on its platform. Patreon then removed access to PayPal services for creators who produced sexual content.[\[214\]](#cite_note-214)

If PayPal determines an account has been used for fraud, for another purpose that violates PayPal's Acceptable Use Policy, or without the account holder's authorization, PayPal puts limitations on, or freezes, the account.[\[215\]](#cite_note-215) PayPal has been involved in several public controversies or disputes after freezing the account of high-profile users such as [Richard Kyanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kyanka "Richard Kyanka")—then-owner of the website [Something Awful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Something_Awful "Something Awful")—in September 2005,[\[216\]](#cite_note-216) [Cryptome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptome "Cryptome") in March 2010,[\[217\]](#cite_note-217)[\[218\]](#cite_note-218)[\[219\]](#cite_note-219) and [April Winchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Winchell "April Winchell")—the owner of [Regretsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regretsy "Regretsy")—in December 2011. The account was reinstated, and PayPal apologized and donated to her cause.[\[220\]](#cite_note-220) In September 2010, PayPal froze the account of [Markus Persson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_Persson "Markus Persson"), a [Minecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minecraft "Minecraft") developer. Persson publicly said that he had not received a clear explanation from PayPal as to why the account was frozen, and that PayPal was threatening to keep the money in the account if it found any suspicious activity. His account contained around €600,000 (US$655,842).[\[221\]](#cite_note-rock-paper-shotgun-221)

PayPal's partner [MasterCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MasterCard "MasterCard") ceased taking donations to [WikiLeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks "WikiLeaks") in 2010, and after the [U.S. State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_State "United States Department of State") said WikiLeaks was engaged in illegal activities, PayPal followed suit by suspending—and later permanently restricting—payments to the website.[\[222\]](#cite_note-222) [Hacktivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacktivist "Hacktivist") supporters of WikiLeaks retaliated by coordinating [cyber attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberattack "Cyberattack") on PayPal, MasterCard, and other companies that took action against WikiLeaks.[\[223\]](#cite_note-223)

In February 2011, PayPal [unbanned](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/unbanned "wikt:unbanned") the account of a website that supports [Iraq War resisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Iraq_War_resisters "List of Iraq War resisters") after it had enough information to fulfill its [know your customer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Know_your_customer "Know your customer") guidelines. The [Chelsea Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Manning "Chelsea Manning") Support Network claimed the backdown was a reaction to a petition to the company to reinstate the account.[\[224\]](#cite_note-224)

In May 2013, PayPal declined to pay a reward offered in its Bug Bounty Program[\[225\]](#cite_note-225) to a 17-year-old German student who had reported a [cross-site scripting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-site_scripting "Cross-site scripting") flaw on its site.[\[226\]](#cite_note-226) The company wrote that the vulnerability had been previously reported, and chastised the youth for disclosing the issue to the public, but, uniquely, sent him a "Letter of recognition" for the discovery.[\[227\]](#cite_note-227)

In August 2013, entrepreneurs who had used PayPal to collect the funds they raised on [crowdfunding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdfunding "Crowdfunding") platforms like [Kickstarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickstarter "Kickstarter") and [Indiegogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiegogo "Indiegogo") reported difficulty in being able to withdraw the money. Startups included [Proton Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_Mail "Proton Mail"), [Ouya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouya "Ouya"), GlassUp (a rival to [Google Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Glass "Google Glass")), and [Mailpile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mailpile "Mailpile").[\[228\]](#cite_note-228)[\[229\]](#cite_note-229)[\[230\]](#cite_note-230) This also extended to crowdfunded games like [Yatagarasu: Attack on Cataclysm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yatagarasu_Attack_on_Cataclysm "Yatagarasu Attack on Cataclysm"), [Skullgirls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skullgirls "Skullgirls") and [Dreamfall Chapters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamfall_Chapters "Dreamfall Chapters").[\[231\]](#cite_note-231)

In May 2014, PayPal blocked the account of a Russian human rights organisation "RosUznik", which supported political prisoners [arrested at Bolotnaya Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolotnaya_Square_case "Bolotnaya Square case").[\[232\]](#cite_note-Global_Voices-232)[\[233\]](#cite_note-233)

As of January 2015, a class-action lawsuit against PayPal was filed in Israel,[\[234\]](#cite_note-autogenerated2-234) claiming that it arbitrarily froze accounts and held funds for up to 180 days without paying interest and thereby directly profited from it. In the examples given in the lawsuit, PayPal had received complaints against small vendors which were later resolved, but the PayPal account was closed. The lawsuit requests that PayPal be declared a monopoly and thus regulated accordingly.

In April 2015, [The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian") reported that PayPal had blocked the account of [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London")\-based human rights group [Justice for Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_for_Iran "Justice for Iran").[\[235\]](#cite_note-235)

In May 2015, PayPal blocked an account intended to raise money for the production of print copies of [Boris Nemtsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Nemtsov "Boris Nemtsov")'s investigative report "[Putin. War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putin._War "Putin. War")" on Russian military involvement in Ukraine.[\[236\]](#cite_note-236)[\[237\]](#cite_note-237) The explanation by PayPal was that "PayPal does not offer the opportunity to use its system for collecting funds to finance the activities of political parties or for political aims in Russia", though PayPal's _Acceptable Use Policy_ does not mention financing for political goals.[\[232\]](#cite_note-Global_Voices-232) [Non-governmental organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organization "Non-governmental organization") [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House "Freedom House") issued a statement by Susan Corke, director or Eurasia Programs, saying,

> One of the Russian government’s most effective tactics for stifling criticism of President Putin and the government’s human rights abuses is to accuse critics of conducting ‘political activities’ and claiming them to be improper or illegal. It is disturbing, to say the least, that PayPal has supported those tactics by denying individuals the right to help pay for publishing ‘Putin. War,’ the work of murdered opposition figure Boris Nemtsov. PayPal should immediately lift this ban, to help, rather than hinder, press freedom in Russia.[\[238\]](#cite_note-238)

In September 2015, PayPal reappeared as a payment processor at some newly legalized US [online gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_gambling "Online gambling") sites operating in the state of [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey").[\[239\]](#cite_note-239) Since the May 14, 2018, [Supreme Court of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States") overturn of the [Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act of 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_and_Amateur_Sports_Protection_Act_of_1992 "Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act of 1992"), PayPal has been widely available to customers in states where sports betting has been legalized for both depositing and withdrawing money.[\[240\]](#cite_note-240)

By 2016, [ConsumerAffairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConsumerAffairs "ConsumerAffairs") had received over 1,200 consumer reviews of PayPal, resulting in an overall satisfaction rating of one star out of five for the company.[\[241\]](#cite_note-241) Consumers have also launched numerous anti-PayPal Facebook pages and Twitter accounts to air their complaints.[\[242\]](#cite_note-242)

In February 2017, PayPal froze the account of [News Media Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Media_Canada "News Media Canada"), a Canadian [trade association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_association "Trade association"), in response to a payment from _[The Reminder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reminder_\(Flin_Flon\) "The Reminder (Flin Flon)")_, a [Flin Flon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flin_Flon,_Manitoba "Flin Flon, Manitoba"), [Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba "Manitoba") [community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_journalism "Community journalism") [newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper "Newspaper"), that was intended to cover the fee for _The Reminder_'s submission of articles for consideration in a nationwide journalism contest run by News Media Canada, including one discussing [Syrian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_Syrian_Civil_War "Refugees of the Syrian Civil War"). PayPal cited United States regulations as a reason for flagging the transaction between Canadian entities.[\[243\]](#cite_note-243)

In September 2018, PayPal banned radio host [Alex Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Jones "Alex Jones") and his website [InfoWars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InfoWars "InfoWars"), saying that his site has content that is hateful and discriminatory against certain religious groups.[\[244\]](#cite_note-244)

PayPal discontinued payments to [Pornhub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornhub "Pornhub") models on November 14, 2019,[\[245\]](#cite_note-245) alleging that "Pornhub has made certain business payments through PayPal without seeking our permission".[\[246\]](#cite_note-Vice_Pornhub-246) Pornhub criticized the decision as one that affected "over a hundred thousand performers who rely on them for their livelihoods" and steered its payees toward other payment options.[\[246\]](#cite_note-Vice_Pornhub-246)[\[247\]](#cite_note-247)

In September 2020, PayPal issued new terms of service which introduced a fee for inactive accounts in 19 countries.[\[248\]](#cite_note-248)[\[249\]](#cite_note-249) PayPal sent its clients an email about the updated terms, but did not mention introducing such a fee.\[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

PayPal faces criticism over its policy requiring that the name on a user account must match the account holder's legal name, and that any user seeking to change the name on their account must provide government-issued photo identification. Trans and nonbinary users and rights advocates object because this means that any trans user whose preferred name differs from their legal name is forced to use their [deadname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadname "Deadname") instead. They say this is not only disrespectful, but also endangers trans users, because the name on the PayPal account is revealed to the payee whenever a user makes a payment, thereby [outing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outing "Outing") the user and potentially exposing them to people who may harass or injure them. By contrast, other financial companies, such as MasterCard, have made it easier for trans and nonbinary users to change the names on their accounts.[\[250\]](#cite_note-250)

In July 2021, PayPal announced a plan to collaborate with the [Anti-Defamation League (ADL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League "Anti-Defamation League") Center on Extremism, the [League of United Latin American Citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_United_Latin_American_Citizens "League of United Latin American Citizens"), and several other nonprofits to analyze its users' transactions in order to investigate the finances of extremist and hate groups in the United States, and to share the results with law enforcement, policymakers, and other financial corporations.[\[251\]](#cite_note-paypalADL-251)[\[252\]](#cite_note-reutersADL-252) The ADL's CEO, [Jonathan Greenblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Greenblatt "Jonathan Greenblatt"), stated that this initiative is meant to help "mitigat\[e\] extremist threats".[\[252\]](#cite_note-reutersADL-252)

In September 2022, PayPal closed the accounts of the British social commentator [Toby Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Young "Toby Young") and two connected organisations: the [Free Speech Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Speech_Union "Free Speech Union") and [The Daily Sceptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Sceptic "The Daily Sceptic") website. PayPal said that it closed the accounts because of breaches of its acceptable use policy, alleging [misinformation about COVID-19 vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine_misinformation_and_hesitancy "COVID-19 vaccine misinformation and hesitancy").[\[253\]](#cite_note-253) PayPal reversed the decision a few days later.[\[254\]](#cite_note-254)[\[255\]](#cite_note-255)

In October 2022, PayPal published an update to its Acceptable Use Policy that included a $2,500 fine for the accounts of users PayPal deemed to have been promoting misinformation. Former PayPal president [David A. Marcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_A._Marcus "David A. Marcus") criticized the change, [tweeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") that "PayPal's new AUP goes against everything I believe in". [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk"), co-founder of X.com—which merged with Confinity to form PayPal—also criticized the change. Following media scrutiny and criticism on social media, PayPal removed the updated policy from its website, stating, "An AUP notice recently went out in error that included incorrect information. PayPal is not fining people for misinformation and this language was never intended to be inserted in our policy. Our teams are working to correct our policy pages."[\[256\]](#cite_note-256)[\[257\]](#cite_note-257)

In March 2002, two PayPal account holders separately sued the company for alleged violations of the [Electronic Funds Transfer Act (EFTA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Funds_Transfer_Act "Electronic Funds Transfer Act") and California law. Most of the allegations concerned PayPal's dispute resolution procedures. The two lawsuits were merged into one class-action lawsuit (In re: PayPal litigation). An informal settlement was reached in November 2003, and a formal settlement was signed on June 11, 2004. The settlement requires that PayPal change its business practices (including changing its dispute resolution procedures to make them EFTA-compliant) and make a US$9.25 million payment to members of the class. PayPal denied any wrongdoing.[\[258\]](#cite_note-258)

In June 2003, [Stamps.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamps.com "Stamps.com") filed a lawsuit against PayPal and eBay claiming breach of contract, breach of the implied covenants of good faith and fair dealing, and interference with contract, among other claims. In a 2002 license agreement, Stamps.com and PayPal agreed that Stamps.com technology would be made available to allow PayPal users to buy and print postage online from their PayPal accounts. Stamps.com claimed that PayPal did not live up to its contractual obligations and accused eBay of interfering with PayPal and Stamps.com's agreement, hence Stamps.com's reasoning for including eBay in the suit.[\[259\]](#cite_note-nytimesarchive-259)[\[260\]](#cite_note-260)

Craig Comb and two others filed a class action against PayPal in _Craig Comb, et al. v. PayPal Inc._, alleging illegal misappropriation of customer accounts, and detailing their customer service experiences, including the freezure of deposited funds for up to 180 days until dispute resolution by PayPal. PayPal argued that the plaintiffs were required to arbitrate their disputes under the [American Arbitration Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Arbitration_Association "American Arbitration Association")'s Commercial Arbitration Rules. The court ruled against PayPal, stating that "the User Agreement and arbitration clause are substantively unconscionable under California law."[\[261\]](#cite_note-261)

In September 2002, [Bank One Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_One_Corporation "Bank One Corporation") sued PayPal for allegedly infringing its cardless payment system patents.[\[262\]](#cite_note-262) The following year, PayPal countersued, claiming that Bank One's online bill-payment system was an infringement against PayPal's online bill-payment patent, issued in 1998.[\[263\]](#cite_note-263) The two companies agreed on a [settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_\(litigation\) "Settlement (litigation)") in October 2003.[\[264\]](#cite_note-264)

In November 2003, [AT&T Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T_Corporation "AT&T Corporation") filed suit against eBay and PayPal claiming that their payment systems infringed an AT&T patent, filed in 1991 and granted in 1994.[\[265\]](#cite_note-265) The case was settled out of court the following month, with the terms of the settlement undisclosed.[\[266\]](#cite_note-266)

In June 2011, PayPal and [Israel Credit Cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Credit_Cards "Israel Credit Cards") (CAL)—a credit card issuer subsidiary of [Israel Discount Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Discount_Bank "Israel Discount Bank")—were sued for [NIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Israeli_Shekel "New Israeli Shekel") ₪16 million. The claimants accused PayPal of deliberately failing to notify its customers that CAL was illegally charging them for currency conversion fees.[\[267\]](#cite_note-267)

A class-action lawsuit filed in 2010, in which the plaintiffs contested PayPal's "holds" on funds, was settled in 2016.

PayPal has proposed a settlement in the amount of US$3.2 million in _Zepeda v. PayPal_ which has yet to be ratified. As part of the settlement proposal, the company agreed to change some of its policies.[\[268\]](#cite_note-268)

In 2017, following a class action lawsuit between PayPal and its users over unexplained account closures, a settlement agreement was reached—and granted final approval by a federal judge—in which PayPal agreed to pay the class US$4 million, modify the disclosure of its reserve and hold practices, and clarify its dispute resolution process.[\[269\]](#cite_note-269)

In January 2022, a class-action lawsuit was filed against PayPal that claims the company froze accounts without providing adequate explanation, or for arbitrary reasons, and that PayPal told account holders they would "have to get a subpoena" to learn why their accounts were frozen. The lawsuit alleges that PayPal also confiscated funds for itself after 180 days, violating both the [RICO Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racketeer_Influenced_and_Corrupt_Organizations_Act "Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act") and PayPal's own policies.[\[270\]](#cite_note-270)[\[271\]](#cite_note-271)

In March 2026, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission "Federal Trade Commission") issued a warning against PayPal and three other payment processing brands for debanking of law-abiding citizens over political, religious or certain law-abiding activities.[\[272\]](#cite_note-272)

On 21 May 2015, PayPal agreed that PayPal Credit would pay a US$25 million fine to settle a complaint filed in federal court by the [U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Financial_Protection_Bureau "Consumer Financial Protection Bureau").[\[120\]](#cite_note-cfpb-120) The complaint alleged that consumers using PayPal were signed up for PayPal credit accounts without their knowledge or consent, that PayPal had promised discounts and payment options the consumers never received, and that users trying to sign up for the regular, non-credit, PayPal accounts were signed up for credit accounts instead.[\[120\]](#cite_note-cfpb-120)[\[273\]](#cite_note-wp-273) The complaint was filed in the [United States District Court for the District of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_District_of_Maryland "United States District Court for the District of Maryland"), which ordered PayPal Credit to refund $15 million to consumers and to pay a $10 million fine.[\[120\]](#cite_note-cfpb-120)
