_**Musk v. Altman**_ was a February 2024 lawsuit in the [United States District Court for the Northern District of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Northern_District_of_California "United States District Court for the Northern District of California") in which [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") accused [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI") and its executives, including CEO [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman "Sam Altman"), of violating the company's founding agreement by prioritizing profits over [AI safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_safety "AI safety").

On May 18, 2026, the jury decided in favor of OpenAI and its executives, finding that Musk's claims were barred by the [statute of limitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_limitations "Statute of limitations").[\[5\]](#cite_note-:1-5)

Musk and Altman are two of the eleven co-founders of OpenAI, which was founded in 2015.[\[6\]](#cite_note-6)

In early 2018, Musk proposed taking direct control of [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI"), either through a majority stake in a for-profit subsidiary or by folding the organization into [Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla,_Inc. "Tesla, Inc."). The other co-founders, including [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman "Sam Altman"), [Greg Brockman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Brockman "Greg Brockman"), and [Ilya Sutskever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Sutskever "Ilya Sutskever"), rejected the proposal.[\[7\]](#cite_note-7) Musk left the OpenAI board in February 2018, publicly citing a potential conflict of interest with Tesla's growing artificial intelligence work, although later reporting indicated the underlying dispute concerned control of the organization.[\[8\]](#cite_note-8)

The lawsuit was originally filed on February 29, 2024.[\[9\]](#cite_note-9)[\[10\]](#cite_note-10)[\[11\]](#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](#cite_note-12)

In November 2024, Musk filed a motion for [preliminary injunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preliminary_injunction "Preliminary injunction") to block OpenAI from changing from a nonprofit to a for-profit. He believes the conversion violates the terms of his contributions, which total $44 million, to OpenAI from 2016 to 2020.[\[13\]](#cite_note-13) In February 2025, United States District Judge [Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvonne_Gonzalez_Rogers "Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers") said that Musk's claim of irreparable harm was a stretch.[\[14\]](#cite_note-14)

In April 2025, twelve former employees of OpenAI stated in an amicus brief that OpenAI had abandoned its nonprofit roots and that Altman "was a person of low integrity who had directly lied to employees about the extent of his knowledge and involvement in OpenAI’s practices of forcing departing employees to sign lifetime non-disparagement agreements." The motion was filed by Harvard law professor [Lawrence Lessig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Lessig "Lawrence Lessig").[\[15\]](#cite_note-15)

In April 2025, OpenAI countersued Musk, claiming that his actions were deliberate tactics to slow OpenAI in order to benefit his own interests.[\[16\]](#cite_note-16) That month, the California attorney general declined to join Musk's lawsuit.[\[17\]](#cite_note-17)

On May 1, 2025, Judge [Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvonne_Gonzalez_Rogers "Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers") trimmed the lawsuit, excluding claims of false advertising and breach of fiduciary duty, but allowed claims of fraud and unjust enrichment to proceed.[\[18\]](#cite_note-18)

On May 5, 2025, OpenAI announced that it was no longer planning to restructure into a for-profit company separate from its nonprofit board. Later that day, Musk's lawyer [Marc Toberoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Toberoff "Marc Toberoff"), announced that the lawsuit against OpenAI would continue.[\[19\]](#cite_note-19) In October 2025, OpenAI formed a for-profit company – OpenAI Group PBC – with the original non-profit receiving 26 percent ownership in the new entity and Microsoft receiving a 27 percent stake.[\[20\]](#cite_note-20)

As part of the case files disclosed by Musk's lawyers in federal court in November 2025, Altman was shown to have reached out to [Shivon Zilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivon_Zilis "Shivon Zilis") on February 9, 2023, asking her advice about whether to publicly praise Musk on X, which he later did.[\[21\]](#cite_note-21)

In April 2026, Musk amended the lawsuit asking that any monetary damages he might be awarded be given to OpenAI's charity and that Altman be removed from OpenAI's board.[\[22\]](#cite_note-22)

In addition to Altman, various other OpenAI executives, including OpenAI president [Greg Brockman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Brockman "Greg Brockman"), were sued by Musk in the lawsuit as well.[\[24\]](#cite_note-lawsuit-24)

[Jury selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jury_selection "Jury selection") for the case began on April 27, 2026.[\[24\]](#cite_note-lawsuit-24)

On April 28, 2026, Elon Musk began his testimony against Altman and OpenAI, lasting until April 30;[\[25\]](#cite_note-25)[\[26\]](#cite_note-26) he was also questioned by both OpenAI's attorney [William Savitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Savitt "William Savitt")[\[27\]](#cite_note-:0-27) and by Musk's own lawyer [Steven Molo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Molo?action=edit&redlink=1 "Steven Molo (page does not exist)").[\[28\]](#cite_note-28) The next witness on Musk's side, after Musk himself finished testifying, was [Jared Birchall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Birchall "Jared Birchall").[\[27\]](#cite_note-:0-27) Molo also asked Rogers what another key witness, [Stuart J. Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_J._Russell "Stuart J. Russell"), can discuss on the stand; Rogers replied that [existential risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_risk "Existential risk") is outside the scope of the trial.[\[27\]](#cite_note-:0-27)

[Shivon Zilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivon_Zilis "Shivon Zilis") was another witness on the stand.[\[29\]](#cite_note-29) Zilis, a former member of OpenAI's board, had children with Musk. This fact was confidential before it was made public by [Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider "Business Insider") in 2022.[\[30\]](#cite_note-:2-30)

On May 11, Microsoft CEO [Satya Nadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satya_Nadella "Satya Nadella") testified that Musk never expressed concern to him that Microsoft's investments in OpenAI violated any terms or commitments.[\[31\]](#cite_note-31)

On the last day of testimony, Molo called Savitt's focus "ironic" as they argued on the accuracy of each side's statements. Gonzalez Rogers intervened, saying that "This entire trial is a giant irony."[\[30\]](#cite_note-:2-30)

On May 18, 2026, the jury decided in favor of Altman and OpenAI, finding that Musk's claims were barred by the [statute of limitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_limitations "Statute of limitations").[\[5\]](#cite_note-:1-5)
